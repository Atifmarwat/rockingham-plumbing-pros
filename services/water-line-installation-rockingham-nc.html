Types of Water Line Installation

Depending on the property, there are different ways of installing the water line. This will depend on how far the water supply is from the property, how many fixtures there are, expected water requirements and property conditions.

Main Water Supply Line

A main water supply line will connect the approved water connection to the plumbing within the property. When planning the installation, you should consider how long the line will be, water pressure available, how many fixtures are required and what plumbing is required within the property. It is important to know the difference between the main water supplied by the utility and your private service line.

Underground Water Line

Many water service lines will be installed underground. This will usually involve planning the route, finding any underground utilities, preparing the ground and laying the pipe, with connections. Underground water line installation will need to take account of the site conditions.

The route of the pipe should be planned around any existing structures, driveways, garden, trees, drainage and underground utilities. The depth, bed and protection of the pipe should follow any requirements and the material required.

Domestic Water Line

A domestic water line will provide the connection to your home. This can include new builds or improving your plumbing. The line should be the correct size to provide the expected water requirements.

When planning you may want to consider how many bathrooms, kitchens, laundry connections, outside taps and other fixtures you have. The distance from the connection and pressure can also affect the size of the pipe. Planning a domestic water line installation will help ensure you have the correct water supply to your plumbing fixtures.

Commercial Water Line

A commercial water line may be larger and have different requirements depending on the number of fixtures. Offices, retail units, restaurants and other commercial buildings can all have different plumbing requirements.

When planning the installation you should consider what the building will be used for and any plumbing requirements. You may also need to work with contractors, engineers, property managers and your local water supplier.

New Builds

A new build provides an opportunity to plan the water service line. This can help avoid any issues with landscaping, paving and other developments.

You should also consider when the water line will be installed to avoid any clashes with foundations, electrics, drainage and other underground utilities.

Choosing the Correct Material

The pipe should be the correct material for the application. This can depend on if it is being installed internally or underground, local requirements, water conditions, connection and manufacturers requirements.

Common Types of Pipes

  • PEX pipes – Flexible plastic pipes commonly used in many domestic water distribution applications. Due to being flexible they can be easier to route.
  • Copper pipes – Commonly used for plumbing. Can be the correct option for some water supply applications.
  • Approved Plastic Water Service Pipes – There are certain plastic pipes which are specifically for water service. Underground water lines will need to use an approved product.

Not all plumbing pipes will be suitable for all water applications. Just because the pipe is for internal distribution does not mean it will be suitable for underground. Check the rating of the material, fittings, connection type and installation instructions.

Correct Size of Pipe

The size of the pipe will affect how much water can pass through. If the water service line is too small it can cause low pressure or insufficient flow when multiple fixtures are being used. However, using a pipe that is too large can cause unnecessary costs for extra materials and installation.

When deciding on the size of the pipe you should consider the expected requirements of the property, length of pipe, available water pressure, fixtures and any plumbing requirements. The correct size should be decided based on the actual project.

Important Considerations

To ensure a successful installation you should consider the property and connection requirements before digging. This can help avoid any unnecessary changes.

Water Source and Connection

The first thing you should consider is where the water is coming from and where the new line can be connected. If you are using a public water supply, your water supplier can tell you how to connect, what meter is required and what is public and what is private.

The route should be planned to ensure it is practical to connect. You should also consider where the pipe will enter the building and connect to the plumbing.

Underground Utilities

If you are installing the pipe underground you may need to go over electrical cables, gas pipes, sewage pipes, communication lines or existing water supplies. These will need to be found using the correct locating method.

Other considerations can include;

  • Difficult ground (rocky).
  • Trees and garden.
  • Existing concrete or paving.
  • Driveways and pathways.
  • Slopes and drainage.
  • Limited access for digging.

This can affect how the pipe is routed, what method is used to dig, what equipment is required and how the site is reinstated.

Permissions, Inspections and Utilities

Some water lines will require permissions, inspections or approval from your local authority or water supplier.

Before starting the installation check if you will need an approved connection, inspection or specific materials. Your property owner should also know what parts need to be completed by the utility.

Water Line Installation Process

Every property will be different but a typical installation can involve the following key points.

Property Considerations

Firstly you should consider the water requirements of the property, connection, route and site conditions. This can include how far the property is away, how many fixtures are required, equipment access and any underground obstructions.

The installation should include what material to use, size of pipe, connections, digging and any approvals.

Digging

Once you have decided on the route and any approvals you can start to prepare the installation area. Depending on the property and approved method you may need to dig a trench.

The pipe should be laid following the planned route. The pipe should be supported, aligned and protected. You should be careful when working around any existing utilities and nearby structures. The fittings and connections should be the correct type for the pipe and water service.

Connections and Testing

Once the pipe is in place you can connect it to the water supply and plumbing. Ensuring you have the correct connections can help avoid any leaks or compatibility issues.

Depending on the project, you may need to test and inspect. This can include checking for any leaks in connections, ensuring the system is working and completing any inspections. The installation should not be put into service until all the checks have been completed.

Reinstatement

Once all the checks have been completed you can backfill the hole with the correct materials. You may need to level the area.

If the installation is effecting a driveway, concrete surface, garden or other developments you should discuss reinstatement. Your property owner should know what will be included within the installation.

FAQs

How Long Will the Installation Take?

The installation time can depend on how long the pipe is, digging requirements, site access, connection requirements and inspections. A straightforward installation can be quicker than one with difficult ground, utility co-ordination and reinstatement. We can discuss the expected timescale once we have viewed the property.

What is the Best Type of Pipe?

There is no one best type of pipe for all installs. PEX, copper and approved plastic water service pipes can all be suitable for different applications. Consider where the installation will be, water conditions, local requirements and what fittings/connections are required.

How Deep Should the Water Service Be?

The depth of the water service can depend on what codes are required, local conditions, utility requirements and where it is being installed. Factors such as protection from freezing, nearby underground utilities and exposure can affect how deep it should be. Ensure you know the correct depth for your property.

Do I Require Any Permissions?

Some installations will require permissions, inspections or approval from the relevant authority or water utility. This can depend on what type of installation and connection you are doing.

Who Owns the Water Line Between the Meter and House?

This can depend on your water supplier and what service you have. Some parts may be owned by the utility whilst others by your property. Contact your local water utility to find out what is owned by whom.

Can I Install an Underground Water Line with Minimal Digging?

This can depend on the layout of the property, ground conditions, access, existing structures and what method can be used. Some installations can be less intrusive whilst others will require an open trench. By viewing the property we can help decide what is the best option.

What Affects the Cost of Installing a New Water Line?

The main factors will be how long the pipe is, size of pipe, pipe material, digging requirements, connection requirements, permissions, inspections and reinstatement. Once we understand what is required and the conditions of the property we can give an accurate cost.
