Signs Your Water Heater Needs Maintenance

If you are experiencing changes in the performance of your water heater then it may be time to have an inspection.

Look out for:

  • Fluctuating Water Temperatures – Water may be too hot, too cold or fluctuating.
  • Noises – Gurgling, popping or knocking noises could be down to sediment build-up or issues with how it is running.
  • Slow Hot Water Recovery – The unit may be taking longer than usual to produce hot water.
  • Discolouration – If your hot water is rusty or cloudy it could be a sign of corrosion, sediment build up or an issue with your plumbing.
  • Unusual Smell – If there is a constant smell coming from your hot water it should be investigated.
  • Visible Water – If there is water around the tank, valves or connections it could be a sign of a leak.

These issues will not all be due to the same cause. A professional can inspect your equipment and advise on any cleaning, adjustments, repairs or replacements.

What Will Be Done During Professional Water Heater Maintenance?

Depending on what type of system you have (traditional storage tank or tankless), what the manufacturer says and how the equipment is in overall condition a professional will inspect your system. They will generally look at:

Inspection of Tank and Connections

The technician will inspect your water heater, visible pipework, shut off valve, drain valve and surrounding area. This can identify any issues such as corrosion, water leaks, loose connections, damaged insulation.

The surrounding area is also important to check. There may be items stored around the appliance, limited access or signs of water damage.

Sediment Build Up

Over time minerals and particles can build up inside your storage water heater. Depending on the water supply and usage this can cause issues with heating and noise.

If appropriate the tank can be drained and flushed to remove build up. This should be done in accordance with the manufacturers guidelines and appropriate safety measures. If the tank is heavily corroded or has not been looked after it may need to be assessed first.

Anode Rod

Many traditional water heaters will have a sacrificial anode rod. This helps to protect the tank from corrosion. Over time this will deteriorate.

During appropriate maintenance the condition can be checked to see if it should be replaced. If the anode rod is empty then the tank may be more susceptible to corrosion. However, the correct decision will depend on what the manufacturer says and how the anode rod is in condition.

Temperature and Pressure Relief Valve

The temperature and pressure relief valve (T&P Valve) is an important safety feature. It will release water if the temperature or pressure inside the tank becomes unsafe.

A professional can inspect the valve and pipework to see if there are any visible issues, corrosion or incorrect fitting. If the valve looks damaged then it should be checked. Never block the discharge pipe.

Inspect Heating and Controls

During maintenance the accessible controls and heating parts can be checked. This may include checking the thermostat, electric heating elements, gas burner and connections.

This will help identify any signs of incorrect running or deterioration. Any electrical, gas or internal adjustments should be done by an appropriately qualified professional.

Gas or Electric Water Heater Maintenance

Both gas and electric water heaters will have similar maintenance requirements however the heating process will differ. The requirements for gas water heater maintenance and electric hot water heater maintenance will depend on the type of equipment and its condition.

Gas Water Heater

Gas water heaters have a burner which heats the water inside the tank. During maintenance the burner, ignition parts, gas connections, burning air and venting can be checked.

Venting is very important to ensure the combustion gases are being released safely. If there are any issues with the venting, burner running incorrectly or signs of issues with the combustion then a professional should carry out the work. Never tamper with gas connections or burner parts unless appropriately qualified.

Electric Water Heater

Electric water heaters usually have one or more heating elements controlled by thermostats. During maintenance, the accessible connections can be checked. The heating elements and controls can be checked to see if they are running correctly.

If you are running out of hot water quickly or experiencing fluctuating temperatures then the heating elements or thermostat may need looking at. Always ensure the power is turned off before working on the equipment. Homeowners should never open up access panels or tamper with internal wiring.

Tankless Water Heater

Tankless water heaters heat the water as it passes through the unit. Despite the differences they will still need regular maintenance. Tankless water heater maintenance and tankless hot water heater maintenance can help identify mineral build up and other issues that may affect performance.

Descale

Minerals can build up inside the heat exchanger of your tankless water heater. If you live in an area with minerals in the water this can cause restricted water flow and issues with how effectively it is heating.

Depending on what the manufacturer recommends and the water conditions the heat exchanger may need descaling or flushing with an approved cleaning solution. This will depend on the model.

Filters, Venting and Controls

A technician may inspect the accessible inlet filters, water connections, venting and controls. Error codes, fluctuating temperatures and reduced hot water flow can help identify where issues may be.

Tankless water heaters should not be treated the same as traditional tanks. A professional tankless water heater maintenance service can check the system according to the manufacturer's recommendations.

How Often Should My Water Heater Be Serviced?

Depending on the model, water quality, household usage and what the manufacturer recommends many homeowners will have an inspection once a year. Some may need it more or less frequently.

A technician can help you find out how often the equipment should be serviced. If you are using a lot of hot water or noticing mineral build up then it may need more frequent maintenance.

Ensure you have the owners manual and any service history. This can help a professional understand what specifications the unit has, what maintenance has been done and what should be done.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should my Water Heater Be Serviced?

Many homeowners will have a professional inspect their water heater once a year. However, this will depend on the model, water quality, household usage and what the manufacturer recommends. A technician can advise you on how often your equipment should be serviced.

Should my water heater be flushed every year?

Not all water heaters will need to be flushed every year. This will depend on how much sediment has built up and what the manufacturer recommends. A professional can advise if it should be flushed and how it can be done safely.

What happens if I don’t remove the sediment?

Sediment can cause gurgling noises, issues with heating and less efficient heating in storage water heaters. This will depend on how much sediment there is, what type and how the tank is.

Is there a difference between gas and electric water heater maintenance?

Yes. Both may need connections, valves and general inspection but gas will also need burner checks and venting. Electric will have heating elements and electrical controls.

Do tankless water heaters need maintenance?

Yes. They may need to be descaled, filters cleaned and connections, venting and controls checked. This will depend on the model and mineral content of the water.

Can my water heater be fixed during maintenance?

This will depend on why it is not producing hot water. If the thermostat, heating element, burner, electrical or gas issues then it may need repairing. An inspection will be needed to identify the issue.

Should I have an older water heater serviced?

An older water heater can still benefit from an inspection. However, it will not stop corrosion or help a tank that is deteriorating. A professional can inspect the condition and advise if it is better to continue with maintenance, repair or replace.
