When Should You Replace Your Water Heater?

You may start to notice issues with your water heater and it is beginning to near the end of its lifespan. Common problems include:

Leaking Tank & Corrosion

If you are noticing water coming from the bottom of your storage water heater this could be due to leaking connections, drain valves or T&P valves. However, if the water is coming from the tank itself this could be due to corrosion.

Generally if there is a lot of damage to the inside of the tank it will not be possible to replace the connection and so it will be more practical to replace the whole water heater.

If you are seeing rust on any visible connections or areas it’s worth looking into. Whilst this doesn’t necessarily mean the tank has failed, it can help identify issues.

Insufficient Hot Water

If you are noticing that your water heater isn’t providing enough hot water for your household this may be due to the system struggling. You may find that you are running out of hot water quicker whilst showering, or you may notice fluctuating temperatures.

This can be caused by a number of issues. It could be caused by component failure, build up of sediment, lower heating or a unit too small for your requirements. If your water heater is older or has had regular issues it may be worth replacing.

Regular Servicing & Strange Noises

If you are regularly having to call out for servicing it can be frustrating. This may mean that your water heater is no longer practical to keep. If you are frequently having to service your water heater.

Strange noises can occur in a storage water heater. A popping or rumbling noise can be caused by build up of sediment at the bottom of the tank. Other noises can be caused by components. Having the unit inspected can help to determine if it is the right option for you.

Choosing Your New Water Heater

When replacing your existing unit you may want to look at what options you have available. Choosing the right unit for your property will depend on what utilities you have, how much hot water you need, how much space you have and your budget.

Traditional Storage Units

A traditional water heater stores hot water in an insulated tank. When you use hot water it will draw from this tank and heat more water.

Storage units come in various sizes and fuel sources. If your household use matches the size of the tank and you have space for the new unit this may be a good option.

Looking at the size, first hour rating, energy efficiency, size and connections can help you choose the right unit. Just because you are replacing with the same gallon size doesn’t mean it will match your household requirements.

Tankless

A tankless water heater will heat the water as it flows through. This can provide instant hot water. Tankless water heater replacement may be an option if you are looking to change your current system.

You may want to consider a tankless unit if you are looking to change your unit, free up more space or look at your long term energy use. However, tankless units will have different installation and maintenance requirements.

The unit needs to be sized to cope with the number of fixtures running at once. You will also want to look at the fuel/energy source, venting, water quality and installation location.

Gas Or Electric

Depending on the type of unit you will need different connections. For a gas unit you will need to have the correct gas supply and approved venting. For an electric unit you will need to have the correct circuit and wiring. Gas water heater replacement will require checking the gas supply and venting, while electric replacements need to match the existing electrical requirements.

You may want to look at what connections you currently have to see if you can replace with something similar without making many changes. Changing the fuel type can cause extra work to be done with the plumbing, electrical, gas and/or venting. Looking at what is required for the whole project can help.

Choosing The Right Size

Ensure you have enough hot water for your household.

Number Of People

Having the right number of people in your home can be a good place to start. However it’s also important to consider your daily habits. If you have a few people showering one after the other you may need something different.

Look at how many bathrooms you have, how often you shower, large baths and hot water using appliances. If you have a few fixtures running at once you will need to ensure the new unit can cope.

First Hour Rating (Storage)

The first hour rating shows how much hot water can be provided in an hour of high use. This looks at how much hot water can be provided from the tank and how well it can heat more.

Tankless units will be looked at based on the flow rate and temperature rise. Choosing a unit that can provide the right amount of water and raise the temperature to what you want. Looking at what your household uses can help avoid issues with the size.

What Is Involved In Replacing Your Water Heater?

When replacing your water heater you will need to ensure it is the right fit for your plumbing, utilities and installation.

Review Existing Unit

Most of the time you will start by looking at your existing unit. The installer may look at what type, size, fuel source, water connections, venting, electrical and drainage you have.

This can help to see if you can have a similar unit fitted with your existing setup. This is important if you are changing the type of unit or moving the unit.

Shutting Down Your Existing Unit

Before removing your existing unit you will need to turn off and isolate your existing system. Follow the manufacturers guidelines and safety procedures. You may need to empty the tank.

Ensure you handle the gas, electrical and water connections correctly. Don’t attempt to disconnect these yourself. You will also need to consider how easily you can access the unit and how heavy it is.

Connecting Up & Fitting New Unit

Once you have looked at the installation area you will need to connect up the new unit. Depending on your chosen unit you may need to make changes to the water connections, valves, gas, electrical, venting and drainage.

Ensure you follow the manufacturers guidelines for fitting the new unit. Ensure you have the correct safety features, spacing and access for future maintenance.

Checking Unit Is Working

Once fitted you will want to ensure everything is working properly. This includes checking for leaks on the water connections, ensuring it is heating properly, temperature settings and any applicable safety features.

Gas units will need to have the correct connections and venting. Electric units will need the correct electricity. Ensure you understand the basics of how to control the unit and what maintenance is recommended.

Replacing Gas Or Electric Unit

Depending on what energy source you currently have you may need to consider:

Gas

When replacing a gas unit you will need to look at the gas connections, burner requirements, venting and combustion air. Whilst the new unit may be gas it may be different to your existing unit.

Don’t assume the existing vent or gas connection will be compatible. Ensure you follow the manufacturers guidelines for the new unit.

Electric

When replacing an electric unit you will need to look at the circuit, wiring, voltage and amps. If the new unit has different electrical requirements you may need to make changes to the circuit or wiring.

You will also need to look at the water connections and any applicable safety features. Ensuring the unit is wired correctly is very important as it combines water, metal and an active heating system.

Changing To Tankless

Changing from a traditional to tankless unit can be a more complex project. You may need to change the gas, increase the electrical, make changes to the water connections, venting and mounting.

You will want to look at how much hot water you need in your property. Choose a tankless unit that can provide enough flow and heat. Ensure the installation location meets the manufacturers requirements and will allow for future service.

You may also want to consider the water quality. Tankless units can be affected by mineral build up so ensure you understand if you should have a filter or regular descaling.

Compare the whole installation and what the benefits would be. The right choice will depend on your property and household.

Taking care of your new water heater can help ensure it is in the right condition. Different units, models and water quality will all have different maintenance requirements. Follow the manufacturers guidelines.

FAQs

How Do I Know When I Need To Replace My Water Heater?

If your tank is leaking, corroding, breaking down regularly and not providing enough hot water it may be worth replacing. Getting it assessed can help you determine if it can be repaired or if it has become too worn.

Should I Replace My Water Heater If It Is Leaking?

It will depend on where the leak is coming from. If it is leaking from a valve or connection it may be able to be repaired. However if it is leaking due to the tank corroding it will generally need replacing.

Can I Replace My Water Heater With A Different Type?

Often yes however if you are changing from tank to tankless or gas to electric you may need to make changes to the utilities, venting and plumbing. Ensure you look at your property before choosing your new unit.

How Long Does It Take To Replace My Water Heater?

This will depend on the system and installation. If it is a simple replacement it may be easier than if you are moving it, changing the fuel type, venting or upgrading the electricity. Your installer can give you a more useful time frame once they have looked at the work.

What Size Should My New Water Heater Be?

This will depend on how many people live in your household, how much hot water you need at peak times and the type of unit. Storage units will depend on the size and first hour rating. Tankless units will depend on the flow rate and temperature rise.

Is A Tankless Unit A Good Option?

It can be a good option for some properties however you will need to ensure it meets your household requirements and works with the utilities you have. Consider the installation, cost, water quality and maintenance.

Do I Need A Permit For My Water Heater?

This will depend on what is required and local regulations. Ask your installer or relevant local building department.
