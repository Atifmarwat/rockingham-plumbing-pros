Sewer Line Installation Rockingham, NC

Having a correctly installed sewer line is essential for moving waste water away from your property. Whether installing a new line, connecting to a plumbing system, moving an existing line, or replacing an incorrect sewer line, you need it to be correctly planned and installed. As well as the correct route, the sewer line needs to be manufactured from the right material, sloped correctly, connected to other points, and have access. Understanding these factors can help property owners make the right choices when having a sewer line installed.

Why Would I Need A New Sewer Line Installed?

You may need to have a new sewer line installed for your property, either because you need to connect to a new waste system, or the existing line isn't suitable for the buildings plumbing. This can vary for each property.

New Builds

New homes and commercial buildings will need a correctly planned waste system. When building, the line will need to be connected to the correct public or private approved system.

The installation would typically consider:

  • Where the building is located,
  • Where the plumbing is located,
  • Where the sewer connection would be,
  • How far away the building is from the connection,
  • What material and diameter pipe would be required,
  • How sloped the pipes would need to be to allow the waste to flow,
  • Ensuring there is access for inspection and maintenance,
  • Permits, inspections and local regulations.

Planning the route of your sewer line can help to avoid any issues with foundations, driveways, gardens, water lines and other underground services.

Replacing Or Moving An Existing Line

Not all sewer installations will be for new builds. Some properties will need a new sewer line installed because the existing pipe has become damaged, collapsed or there are issues with the existing pipe.

Sometimes a new route will be considered for an existing property undergoing renovation, extension or reconfiguration. For example if you are changing the plumbing layout of the building.

Where possible the existing pipe should be assessed to understand what condition it is in. Using a camera system can help to understand how much damage there is and if a complete route needs to be installed.

Planning Your Sewer Line Installation

Planning is one of the most important aspects of your sewer project. You want to ensure it is planned to fit the layout of your property and the waste can be moved correctly down the sewer line.

Connection and Route

Firstly you need to understand where the waste system will be connected from the building. This could be to a public sewer system or approved private waste system.

The route would need to consider:

  • Where the main waste outlet is from the building,
  • Where the connection is and what height it is,
  • Existing underground services,
  • Trees, gardens, driveways, and structures,
  • Where access may be required in the future,
  • Boundaries and any easements.

Try to avoid any unnecessary bends and difficult access. You should know where any existing services are to avoid any disruption to water, electric, gas or communication services.

Property owners should also understand who is responsible for the connection and if you need permission from an utility company.

Slope, Depth and Waste Requirements

The sewer line needs to be sloped correctly to allow the waste to flow. If there isn't enough slope then the waste may not flow quickly and solids could settle. If the slope isn't correct the waste may not flow as expected.

The slope will depend on the size of the pipe, layout, plumbing requirements and heights. You should know what slope is required for your project.

The depth of the pipe is also very important. The pipe should be installed to the correct height considering the connection, ground and other requirements. The height can affect how deep you need to dig, how difficult it is to install and what the overall project involves.

Correct Material

The material for your pipe should be appropriate for underground waste. This can depend on your design, approvals required, pipe size, ground conditions and type of connection.

Common materials used for sewer lines include:

  • PVC – Polyvinyl chloride pipes are often used for many underground drainage projects. This will depend on the product and approved installation requirements.
  • ABS – Acrylonitrile butadiene styrene is used in some drainage systems. This will depend on code and project requirements.
  • Other Approved Materials – Some projects may require other materials depending on the existing system, connection requirements and local requirements.

Ensure you have the correct fittings, joints and connections for the material. Ask what material will be used and why it is appropriate for your sewer line.

Residential And Commercial Sewer Lines

Depending on the size of the building, plumbing requirements, layout and connection requirements your sewer project can vary. Residential sewer lines can be quite straightforward whereas commercial properties can require more planning.

Residential

Residential sewer lines will connect your homes drainage system to the correct waste connection. This can be due to a new property, extension, moved connection or replacement of the existing underground line. Residential sewer line installation rockingham nc may be required for these types of residential projects.

The installation should consider the layout of your homes fittings. These could include toilets, sinks, showers, baths, washing machines and other waste outlets. These are connected to the buildings drainage system and then passed down the sewer line.

As a homeowner you should know where the private sewer line starts and finishes and where it connects to the larger system. You should know what portion of the line you are responsible for.

Commercial

Commercial sewer lines can sometimes be larger and more complex. Offices, retail, restaurants and other commercial buildings can have different waste requirements.

This could require consideration of:

  • Number of plumbing fittings,
  • Amount of waste expected,
  • Pipe sizes and drainage,
  • Connection capacity and location,
  • Access for inspection and maintenance,
  • Consideration for the build schedule and other trades.

The design should be based around the actual requirements of the building. Don't assume a sewer line suitable for one type of property will be suitable for another.

How Will My Sewer Line Be Installed?

Depending on your property and project requirements, your sewer line will be installed in a slightly different way. Below are the typical steps taken.

Initial Survey

An initial survey of the building, existing drainage, connection and access will be carried out. The service provider will understand the layout of your property and any potential issues.

Route Plan

The sewer line, connection points, pipe requirements and areas to dig will be planned. Any underground services should be known.

Permits

Any permits, inspections, utility approvals or connection permissions should be considered. This can depend on the type of project and who is responsible for the sewer.

Digging

The area will be dug to install the sewer line underground. The trench will need to be prepared correctly for the piping.

Installation

The chosen sewer pipe will be installed with the correct fittings. The pipe should be installed to the correct alignment.

Inspections

The installation will be inspected to meet the project requirements. This can include checking connections, alignment and any drainage/leakage tests.

Backfill

Once the checks have been made and permissions obtained the area can be backfilled. Landscaping, paving or other areas disturbed should be considered.

Ask what is included in your quote and if landscaping will be included.

Connecting, Testing And Inspection

Your sewer line won't be useful unless it is connected correctly and drains correctly. The connection from your drain to the underground pipe should be fitted with the correct fittings.

Testing and inspection can help to identify any issues with the installation. This can include:

  • Checking the alignment and slope of the pipe,
  • Checking the joints and connections,
  • Check for any visible leaks or issues,
  • Ensure the waste can flow through the installed line,
  • Complete any required inspections by authorities/building.

A sewer camera can be useful for some projects. This can be used to inspect existing lines or an accessible section of the line. The correct testing will depend on the system.

Property owners should ask what inspections will be carried out and keep any documentation, permissions and project details.

Traditional Or Trenchless

Traditional digging is often used to install a new underground route. This allows the team to gain access to the trench, prepare the bed and install the pipe.

Some projects can be completed using trenchless methods. However this may not always be suitable.

Pipe bursting can be used for some replacement projects. This involves installing a new pipe in the existing route and bursting the old pipe. Pipe lining involves lining the existing pipe. This is usually a remedial option.

The existing pipe, access points, route, nearby services and site conditions will determine if this is suitable. If you need a new route and there isn't a suitable existing pipe then traditional digging may be required.

Ask the service provider to explain what method they will be using, any limitations and how it may affect your property.

Cost Of Installing A Sewer Line Rockingham, NC

The cost of installing your sewer line will depend on the design of your project and site conditions. A short and easily accessible sewer line may be less work than a long, deep sewer line with difficult access and complex connections. When comparing options for Cheap sewer line installation rockingham nc, consider the project requirements and the written quote rather than assuming the lowest price includes everything.

Factors that can influence the cost include:

  • Length of the pipe – The longer the pipe the more fittings and labour will be required.
  • Depth of the trench – The deeper the trench the more work may be required.
  • Material – The material and pipe size will influence the cost of your project.
  • Access – Driveways, buildings, gardens and underground services can make access more complex.
  • Connection – The type and location of the connection can affect the work.
  • Permits – Permissions and inspections can be required.
  • Restoration – Concrete, paving, gardens or other areas may need replacing.

Ask for a written quote outlining the route, material, digging, connection, testing, permits and restoration. Ask if everything is included in the cost or if any items may incur an extra charge.

Common Issues With Sewer Line Installations

Poor planning or installation can cause drainage issues. Some issues may not be noticed until you start using the system.

Avoid:

  • Incorrect slope – The pipe may not slope correctly.
  • Incorrect connections – The pipe may leak or separate.
  • Incorrect material – The pipe and fittings should be suitable for the system and approved requirements.
  • Not planning – Not considering underground services or access can cause issues during installation.
  • No inspections – Not completing the required inspections can cause issues with the installation.
  • Not understanding who is responsible – As a property owner you should know who is responsible for the permits, utility coordination, testing and restoration.

Having a clear installation plan and correct inspections can help to avoid any unnecessary issues.

Caring For Your New Sewer Line

Even though your sewer line has been installed correctly you should still use it sensibly. The way you use it in your home and what goes down the drains can affect how it performs in the long term.

To help avoid any issues:

  • Don't flush wipes, sanitary products, paper towels or anything else inappropriate down the toilet.
  • Don't pour cooking oil or grease down the drains.
  • Use drain strainers where required.
  • Don't ignore slow drains.
  • If several fittings are backing up arrange for an inspection.
  • Ensure cleanouts are accessible.

If you have a new sewer line and are experiencing regular blockages, strange smells or signs of leaking arrange for an assessment. This could be due to an issue with the connection, blockage or other part of the drainage.

FAQ'S

When Would I Need A New Sewer Line Installed?

You may need a new sewer line installed for a new build, extension, moved sewer connection or incorrect existing line. This should be determined by reviewing your property and waste system.

How Long Will My Sewer Line Take To Install?

This will depend on the length of the pipe, depth of the trench, access, connection requirements, permits, inspections and site conditions. A smaller project may be quicker to complete than a longer project with difficult digging and multiple connections. Ask for a time frame for your project.

What Type Of Pipe Will Be Used For My Underground Sewer Line?

PVC is often used for many underground sewer systems. Other approved materials may be suitable for some systems. The correct material will depend on the product, local requirements, pipe size and installation.

Does My Sewer Line Need The Correct Slope?

Yes. The sewer line needs to have the correct slope to allow the waste to flow. The slope will depend on the pipe size, design and plumbing requirements. This should be determined as part of your installation.

Is There A Difference Between Installing And Replacing A Sewer Line?

Yes. Installing a sewer line could mean creating a new route or connection for your property. Replacing means removing or bypassing the existing line and installing a new one. Some replacement projects will use the existing route but others may have a different design.

Can I Have A Sewer Line Installed Without Digging?

Some trenchless methods can be used for certain replacement/remedial projects. However this will not be suitable for all properties. If you need a completely new route then digging may be required.

What Affects The Cost Of Installing My Sewer Line?

The cost will depend on the length, depth, material, digging requirements, connection location, access, permits, testing and restoration. A site assessment and written quote can help you understand what work will be required and how much it will cost.

Do I Need My New Sewer Line Inspected?

Depending on your project and local rules you may need your sewer line inspected/testing. This helps to ensure the pipe, connections and installation meet the correct requirements. Ask what inspections and approvals will be required.
