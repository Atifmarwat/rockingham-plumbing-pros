Pipe Replacement Rockingham NC – Plumbing Pipe Replacement

A plumbing system will have pipes that enable safe and reliable water flow to be maintained throughout a property. If the pipes are very old, constantly leaking or have sustained lots of damage then it may not be the best option to constantly repair. Pipe Replacement Rockingham NC can help replace pipes on your property to ensure you don't have any sections of your plumbing system that are failing. Whether it is a small section of pipe, a water supply or a complete re-pipe of your plumbing system understanding how the current system is will help to decide on the correct solution. Plumbing replacement may be considered when the existing piping is no longer suitable for reliable use.

Why You May Need Pipe Replacement

As the pipes age they may start to deteriorate. In some cases there may be a few issues with the piping on your property. Whereas in other cases there may be multiple plumbing issues meaning that there are wide spread issues with your plumbing.

Constant Leaking

If you have constant leaking on different sections of your plumbing system then it may be worth looking into. If you are having to constantly repair your plumbing then there may be more of an issue than just a faulty fitting.

However if the rest of the pipes in the area are in good condition then a localised repair may be suitable. If there are multiple sections failing then replacing the damaged pipes may be the most suitable option.

Corrosion & Flaking

If you have visible signs of corrosion, flaking or deterioration on your metal pipes then this may mean that the material is beginning to weaken. Sometimes discolouration in your water can be caused by corrosion on parts of your plumbing. However this may also be due to the source of the water and other issues in the plumbing system.

Other signs of issues may include:

  • Rust/corrosion around joints
  • Moisture on multiple sections of the pipe
  • Deteriorated joins
  • Constant leaking around older plumbing
  • Visible cracks or damaged piping

This does not necessarily mean that you will need to completely replace your plumbing. An inspection can help to determine if it is just a section of pipe.

Low Water Pressure

Low water pressure throughout your property may be caused by damaged or blocked water supply piping. If there are multiple issues then it may be worth looking into the overall water supply.

Discolouration, inconsistent water pressure or constant plumbing issues may also be worth investigating. These issues can often have many causes and pipe replacement should only be considered if the current condition of your piping supports this.

Common Reasons to Replace Plumbing Pipes

If the plumbing is no longer functioning reliably or there has been lots of damage to the pipes then it may be necessary to replace them.

Old & Deteriorating Pipes

Different piping will have different characteristics and will have varying lifespans. An older plumbing system may have pipes that are not in great condition.

Just because the pipe is old doesn't mean it needs replacing. The type of pipe, how it was installed, how well it has been looked after and how it looks will all be factored in. An inspection can help to determine if the current pipe is suitable.

Lots of Corrosion & Constant Issues

Corrosion can affect the pipe, joints and connections. If there is a large amount of deterioration then just replacing a small section may not be enough.

If there are constant issues then replacing a larger section of the pipe may be discussed with a plumbing professional. The type of pipe being replaced should be identified.

Damaged, Cracked or Incorrectly Connected Piping

Pipes can become damaged due to impact, movement, incorrect connections and other issues. If there are cracks, separated joints or broken sections then the damaged pipe may need replacing. This can include broken pipes that are no longer suitable for repair.

If only a small section of the pipe is damaged then a partial replacement may be suitable. If there are multiple connected sections with issues then a larger section may be suitable. The type of replacement will depend on where the piping is, what type of pipe and how accessible it is.

Types of Pipe Replacement

There are different types of pipes that may need replacing. Water supply, drainage and underground pipes will all have different purposes.

Water Supply Pipe

Water supply pipes provide clean water to your fixtures and appliances. If there is damage or deterioration to the water supply pipe then there could be leaking, low water pressure or constant plumbing issues. Water pipe replacement may be suitable if the existing supply piping is damaged or deteriorated.

Water supply pipes can be replaced such as;

  • A damaged section of water pipe
  • Bathroom/kitchen pipes
  • Supply piping to fixtures
  • Deteriorated piping within the building
  • Larger section of piping within the property

This should take into consideration the current piping, where it connects, what type of pipe and where the water needs to go. The water supply pipe will also need to be tested.

Drain & Sewer Pipe

Drain pipes carry the waste water from your sink, shower, bath and other fixtures. Sewer pipes carry waste water to the sewer connection point on your property.

Replacing your drain or sewer pipe may be considered if there is cracking, collapse, lots of deterioration or constant issues that can't be resolved by clearing or partial replacement.

Piping replacement is not the same as drain clearing. Piping replacement would be due to actual damage whereas drain clearing would be due to a blockage. A plumbing inspection can help to determine the difference.

Individual Section

Not all plumbing issues will require a full scale replacement. If there is damage to a small section and the rest of the plumbing is in good condition then it may be reasonable to replace just the damaged section.

By partially replacing the piping it will resolve the issue whilst leaving the rest of the plumbing in working order. The piping should be considered with the joints nearby and condition of connected piping. This type of pipe change can be suitable when only one section needs replacing.

Full Scale Re-Pipe

A full scale re-pipe would involve replacing a large section or all of the plumbing supply piping. This may be considered if there are wide spread issues, constant leaks or extensive plumbing issues.

This should be planned out considering where the fixtures are, current piping, access to walls/ceilings and how the water is distributed throughout the property. This can be a larger job than just replacing a section of piping. The scope should be identified.

A full scale re-pipe should only be recommended if the current plumbing supports it. If there is just one leak then this may not be necessary.

Pipe Types

The piping should be a suitable type for the plumbing system, purpose and installation.

Copper Piping

Many water supplies will use copper piping. This is a hard material and will need suitable fittings and installation. When considering copper piping replacement the current connections and suitability of the new piping should be considered.

Depending on the building, plumbing and project it may be suitable to use copper piping.

PEX Piping

PEX piping is a flexible plastic piping and is often used in water supply piping. This can make it easier to run throughout certain areas of the building.

This will require suitable fittings and installation. The piping should be considered with the design, piping specifications and installation requirements.

PVC & Other Drain Piping

PVC piping is often used in certain drainage, waste & venting applications. Older or specialist plumbing systems may have different piping.

The piping should be a suitable type for the purpose of the plumbing. Just because the piping is suitable for drainage doesn't mean it is suitable for potable water. The correct type should be chosen depending on the purpose of the piping.

Pipe Replacement vs Repair

Understanding the current condition of the piping will help decide if pipe replacement or repair is suitable.

Reasons for Pipe Repair

If there is damage to a small section of piping, the rest of the pipe is in good condition, the pipe can be repaired correctly, there is no wide spread deterioration in the plumbing and the piping hasn't constantly failed after previous repair then this may be a suitable option. Replacing the damaged section may be more suitable than replacing the whole system. An inspection can help to determine if it is just a small area of damage.

Reasons for Pipe Replacement

If there is wide spread deterioration, multiple constant leaks, lots of corrosion or major damage then pipe replacement may be considered.

It may also be considered if there are constant repairs being carried out. The size of the section to be replaced can vary from a small section to a larger plumbing system.

The owner should ask why the pipe is being replaced, what section and if a smaller repair would be a reasonable option.

How Pipe Replacement Works

There are generally a few steps to pipe replacement. Depending on whether it is visible piping, hidden piping, underground piping or a larger re-pipe.

Inspection & Planning

An inspection will be carried out to see what the current piping looks like and what the issue is. This may involve considering what type of piping there is, damage, connections, accessibility and condition of surrounding piping.

If there is a larger section to replace then planning will be carried out. This may include identifying where the piping runs and what fixtures it goes to. The scope should include what piping is going to be replaced.

Access & Removal

The damaged or deteriorated piping will need to be accessed. If it is visible piping it can be relatively easy to access. If it is hidden piping then walls, ceilings, floors or other parts of the building may need to be opened up.

This can impact how complex the job is. The scope should identify what surfaces may need to be opened up.

Installation

The new piping will be installed. This will depend on the design and requirements of the piping type. This may include fitting new sections of piping, connecting to existing piping and securing.

Connections should be suitable for the piping type. If there is a larger section to install then the design should be considered with where the fixtures are, piping and accessibility.

Testing

Once installed the plumbing should be checked for any leaks and suitable functioning. The supply piping should be checked to ensure they are working under suitable conditions. Drainage piping should be checked to ensure they are flowing correctly.

Any surfaces that have been opened up may need to be restored. The completion should identify if drywall, painting, flooring or other finishes are included.

What to Expect

There will generally be some disruption to the normal water usage in your home/building. This will depend on where the piping is, how much piping is being replaced and how accessible it is.

If it is just a small section then only the fixtures connected to the piping will be affected. If it is a larger re-pipe then multiple areas may need to be turned off.

Before the work starts it is useful to know:

  • What fixtures/rooms will be affected
  • Will the water need turning off
  • How the new piping will be laid out
  • Will any walls/floors/ceilings need opening up
  • What testing will be carried out
  • Will any cleaning/restoration be included

If the property is occupied planning around bathrooms, kitchens and appliances can help to limit any disruption. If it is a commercial property then it may need to be planned around the business and access to the plumbing.

The time frame will depend on the size and complexity. A small section won't take as long as a full scale re-pipe. Speak directly to the service provider to find out what the expected time frame is.

Cost of Pipe Replacement

The price to replace the piping in Rockingham, NC will depend on the plumbing system and what needs doing. Generally the pipe replacement cost can be identified by assessing the damaged piping, where it is and what piping needs replacing.

Key factors may include:

  • How much piping is being replaced – One section of pipe will be different to replacing the whole supply piping or re-piping.
  • Type of piping – Copper, PEX, PVC and other piping will have different components and installation requirements.
  • Accessibility – Piping behind walls, under floors or underground may require more work to access.
  • Number of fixtures – The number of fixtures, piping and connections can impact how complex the job is.
  • Damage – Corrosion, broken connections and damage to surrounding materials may impact the job.
  • Labour – Removing old piping and fitting new piping may require varying levels of labour.
  • Testing, drywall, flooring etc – Testing may be included.

Before approving the pipe replacement ask for a clear explanation of what work will be done. The estimate should include what sections are being replaced, what type of piping will be used, what access is required and what isn't included.

A larger pipe replacement may require more planning than just a simple repair. Comparing quotes will be most useful if they are similar in the scope of work.

Aftercare

Even though the piping has been replaced it is still a good idea to take reasonable care of them. Whilst you can't prevent all plumbing issues, it can help the property owner to spot any developing issues.

Things to consider after pipe replacement:

  • Check any accessible connections for signs of moisture.
  • Be aware of any unusual pressure changes.
  • Fix any dripping fixtures or leaking valves.
  • Don't put unnecessary pressure on visible piping.
  • Investigate any unexpected water stains or damp.
  • Keep a record of what type of piping and what sections have been replaced.
  • Follow any aftercare instructions.

If there is a new leak then contact the provider to have the area investigated. Don't assume what is causing the issue, it could be an issue with the connections, existing piping nearby or other plumbing.

Keeping a record of the project can be useful for future aftercare. Knowing what type of piping, where it runs and what has been replaced can help with future plumbing.

FAQs

When Should I Replace My Plumbing Pipes?

If there has been lots of damage, constant leaks or the piping has deteriorated then it may be suitable to replace it. The surrounding piping should be assessed to determine what section needs replacing.

Will Pipe Replacement Include a Full Re-Pipe?

This may not be the case. It could just be a small section of damaged piping, water supply, drain or a larger scale plumbing system. A full re-pipe would be a larger service. Speak to the provider.

How Long Will Pipe Replacement Take?

This will depend on how much piping is being replaced, how accessible it is, the layout of the building and whether any restoration is required. A small section will take a lot less time than a full re-pipe. Speak to the provider directly to find out what the expected time frame is.

Can I Replace Old Piping Without Opening Walls?

Some visible piping can be replaced without opening any walls. If it is hidden then walls, ceilings or floors may need to be opened.

What Is the Difference Between Pipe Replacement & Re-Pipe?

Pipe replacement can include replacing a section of damaged piping or specific piping. A re-pipe generally means replacing a large section or all of the piping in the property. The exact scope should be determined.

Is Copper or PEX Best for Replacing Piping?

There isn't a universal answer to this. Copper and PEX will have different characteristics, installation requirements and uses. The correct type will depend on the layout, purpose, compatibility and requirements.

Can Drain Piping Be Replaced?

Yes, if the drain piping has become damaged due to cracking, broken sections or deteriorated connections then it may be replaced. This is different to drain clearing which deals with blocking issues.

What Affects the Cost of a Full Re-Pipe?

This can depend on the size of the property, number of fixtures, type of piping, accessibility, layout, amount of piping being replaced and whether walls or other surfaces need restoring. The estimate should depend on the property and scope.
