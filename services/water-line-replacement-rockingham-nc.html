Water Line Replacement Rockingham, NC

A water line will transport fresh water from the appropriate water supply to your property. This may include underground service line, external piping, valves and internal supply piping.

A water line replacement involves removing part or all of the original piping and fitting new piping to replace the faulty section. Depending on the condition of your piping, the placement of the faulty section and what type of piping you have, you may want to replace just a small area of piping or more.

Property owners may want to consider a water line replacement if they are having frequent leaks, corrosion, cracks or other issues with their current line. Before any work is carried out, an assessment should be carried out to determine which sections of piping would need replacing and whether the line is the property owners responsibility or owned by the water company.

Signs You May Need a Water Line Replacement

Issues with your water line can occur gradually or suddenly. Some signs you may need to inspect your piping further are;

Frequent Leaks

If you are having frequent leaks along the same section of water line, it could be a sign that your piping is deteriorating. If repairs are being made and you are having new leaks in nearby sections then you should consider assessing the overall condition of your water line.

If there is just a small area of damaged piping then you may want to replace just this section. However, if there are multiple areas of deterioration then you may want to consider a larger replacement.

Low Water Pressure

Sometimes a damaged, leaking, blocked or deteriorating water line can cause noticeable low water pressure throughout your property. This could be caused by a faulty valve, fixture or water supply.

If multiple fixtures are affected then you may need to assess your main supply piping. Testing can help determine if it is a water line issue.

Discoloured Water

If you are having brown, yellow or rusty water it could be due to corroding, build up or disturbance within your piping. If you are frequently getting discoloured water then you may need to investigate the condition of your water supply piping.

This doesn't necessarily mean you need to replace your main water line as the issue could be with internal piping, fixtures or water supply.

Wet Areas

If you are noticing unusual wet areas in your soil, constant puddles or wet areas near to where your water line may be underground then it could be due to a leak. Damaged underground piping can cause water to seep into the surrounding soil.

These issues can also be caused by other sources such as leaking irrigation and drainage issues. Correct leak detection can help determine if it is a water line issue.

Old or Deteriorating Piping

Over time old piping can become corroded, with weakened joints, deterioration and frequent failures. Whilst the piping being old does not necessarily mean it needs replacing, the type of piping and condition is important.

An assessment can help determine if your current water line would still be suitable or if replacement should be planned.

Common Reasons for Water Line Replacement

There are a few reasons why you may want to replace your existing water line.

Corrosion and Deterioration

Some piping can deteriorate over time due to corrosion, water and general wear. This can cause leaks to occur in different areas.

If a large section of your piping is deteriorating then it could be more practical to replace.

Ground Movement and Damaged Piping

Underground water lines can become damaged due to ground movement, settling, pressure from surrounding materials or accidental damage. This can cause cracks, misaligned joints or breakages.

The correct replacement should be carried out depending on where the damage is and the conditions of the area. Before any digging is carried out underground utilities should be identified to avoid damage to other services.

Multiple Repairs

If you have had multiple repairs to your water line then you may have wider issues. Repeated failures can cause disruption and make it difficult to rely on your piping.

A professional assessment can help determine how much of your piping is left and how much of a partial or full replacement would be needed.

Old or Inappropriate Piping

Some older plumbing systems may have inappropriate materials. If there have been changes to your building, water demand or plumbing requirements then this could be a reason to consider replacing your current line.

Replacement should be planned taking into consideration the correct materials, sizing, compatibility and plumbing requirements.

Types of Water Line Replacement

Depending on the area of your water supply that needs replacing you may require;

Main Water Line Replacement

Your main water line (often known as your water service line) will transport water from your utility connection or other approved water source to your property. If there is an issue with your main water line it can cause issues with water throughout your building. Main water line replacement could be required depending on the condition of the existing piping.

Main water line replacement could involve removing your existing underground piping and fitting a new service line. Property owners should confirm where the private piping begins and where the water company becomes responsible.

Underground Water Line Replacement

Your underground water line could be running under your garden, landscaping, drive or other areas of your property. If there is a large amount of damage or deterioration to your underground piping then replacement may be required. This could involve digging.

How deep your piping is, the ground conditions, nearby utilities and surface materials could all affect your replacement. Consider how the disturbed areas will be reinstated.

Residential Water Line Replacement

Your residential water line replacement could involve your service line or certain sections of your internal water distribution. This may be required if you have had frequent leaks, deterioration or plumbing upgrades.

Depending on how your home is built, what piping you have, how much water you need and where the piping is located.

Commercial Water Line Replacement

Commercial properties may have a larger water supply due to the size of the building, number of fixtures and how the business operates. If your supply line is failing it can cause issues with multiple rooms, fixtures or business operations.

Consideration should be given to how much water your property needs, pipe sizing, access and any disruption to operations.

Partial Water Line Replacement

Partial replacement involves replacing a specific section of piping. This could be suitable if there is only a small amount of damage and the remaining piping is in a reasonable condition.

Before deciding on this option consider the surrounding piping. If you are connecting new piping to severely deteriorated piping then there could still be issues.

Water Line Materials

Choosing the correct piping material is an important part of your replacement. Different materials will have different installation requirements.

Copper Water Lines

Copper has been used in water supplies for many years. It is a rigid material so will require the correct fittings, support and joining methods.

It could be suitable for certain water service and distribution applications. Consideration should be given to the design of the system, water conditions, installation requirements and any codes.

PEX Water Supply

PEX (cross-linked polyethylene) is a flexible piping material commonly used in water supplies. Its flexibility can be useful for certain areas of piping.

It will require compatible fittings and the correct installation tools. Ensure the product chosen is approved for use and installed correctly according to manufacturers guidelines and requirements.

Other Approved Materials

Depending on the application other approved materials may be considered. Consideration should be given to water pressure, temperature, location and local requirements.

Not all piping materials will be suitable for all areas of your water supply. Determine what the piping will be used for and ensure it meets the requirements.

Water Line Replacement or Repair?

If there is a small amount of damage to your water line and the surrounding piping is in good condition then repairs could be sufficient. However if there is widespread deterioration, multiple recurring leaks, lots of cracking or multiple sections with issues then replacement could be more appropriate. Just because a pipe starts to leak does not always mean that a full replacement will be required.

Consideration should be given to the condition of your existing piping, how reliable a repair would be, access and overall requirements. By having a clear assessment property owners can understand why a certain option is being recommended.

Traditional Digging or Trenchless

Traditional digging can be used to expose your existing water line and remove the faulty piping. This can give direct access to your underground piping however can cause disturbance to your landscaping, ground, concrete or other surfaces.

Trenchless water line replacement may be an option for some underground water line replacements. Depending on the system and site conditions it can reduce the amount of digging required. However it may not be suitable for all piping, properties and damage.

The best option will depend on your existing piping, access points, piping route, surrounding utilities and installation options. An assessment can be carried out to determine what options are available.

Water Line Replacement Process

Depending on your requirements, a typical water line replacement will involve;

  • Initial Assessment – Review the reported issues, property layout and what water supply components are affected.
  • Location of Leak – Identify the issue using suitable diagnostic methods. Assess the existing piping.
  • Planning – Determine the route of the piping, what materials will be used, sizing, access and overall requirements.
  • Utilities and Site – Consider any underground utilities and access.
  • Remove Damaged Piping – Expose and remove the required piping.
  • Install New Piping – Fit suitable replacement piping with compatible connections.
  • Testing – Carry out appropriate testing to check connections, leaks and water flow.
  • Reinstatement – Consider how the disturbed areas will be reinstated.

Depending on your piping, underground service line or larger commercial system the work could be different.

What to Expect from Water Line Replacement

Water line replacement can cause temporary disruption to your water supply. How long this disruption lasts will depend on where the piping is, how it will be installed and how complex the work is.

If you are replacing underground piping then there may be some disturbance to your landscaping or ground. If you are replacing internal piping then there may be some access required through walls, ceilings, floors or utilities.

Before any work is carried out discuss;

  • What areas of your property will be accessed.
  • If your water supply needs to be turned off temporarily.
  • What stages of work will be carried out and how long it may take.
  • If any digging or reinstatement will be required.
  • How your new line will be tested.
  • What approvals may be required.

This will help you prepare for your work.

Maintaining Your New Water Line

A newly installed water line should still receive some consideration. Regularly monitoring can help identify any potential issues.

Consider;

  • Check any accessible connections and exposed piping for any damp or leaks.
  • Monitor for any unusual or frequent changes in water pressure.
  • Monitor for any unusual increase in water usage.
  • Protect any vulnerable piping from freezing.
  • Avoid digging near any buried water lines without checking the utilities.
  • If you are having unusual coloured water or frequent leaks then address this.
  • Follow any maintenance recommendations for the type of piping you have installed.

Ensure you have records of the work carried out. This includes what type of piping has been installed, how it was installed and where the new piping is. This could be useful for future maintenance, improvements or plumbing works.

FAQs – Water Line Replacement Rockingham, NC

When should I replace my water line?

If you have widespread corrosion, frequent leaks, lots of cracking or multiple sections of deterioration then you may want to consider replacement. If there is only a small amount of damage and the rest of your piping is in good condition then targeted repairs could be sufficient. An assessment can help determine the best option.

How long will the water line replacement take?

The length of time will depend on how long your piping is, how deep it is, how accessible it is, how it will be installed and site conditions. A simple replacement may not take as long as a complex replacement involving lots of digging, difficult access or reinstatement.

Will I need digging to replace my main water line?

Generally traditional main water line replacement will require digging. Some projects may be suitable for trenchless methods to reduce the amount of digging however this will depend on your existing piping, property layout and installation conditions.

What type of piping will be used?

Most water supplies will use copper or PEX piping. Other approved materials may be used depending on the application. The correct type will depend on where the piping is, what the system requires, local plumbing regulations and manufacturers guidelines.

Will my water be turned off?

You may need to turn your water off whilst disconnecting your existing piping and fitting your new piping. How long this lasts will depend on your project. Ask what the plans are.

Can I have partial water line replacement?

Yes. If there is only a small amount of damage and the surrounding piping is in a suitable condition then partial replacement could be an option. If there is widespread deterioration then a larger replacement may be more appropriate.

What affects the cost of water line replacement Rockingham, NC?

The cost will depend on how long the piping is, what type of piping, how deep it is, how accessible it is, how much digging, labour, approvals and reinstatement is required. Replacing a small section of piping may be different to replacing your full underground service line. An assessment should be carried out to get an accurate cost for your property.

Who is responsible for replacing my main water line?

This will depend on where the damaged piping is and what the rules are for your water company. Some sections of your water service line may be your responsibility however some components may be owned/maintained by the water company. Confirm who owns the piping before organising replacement.
