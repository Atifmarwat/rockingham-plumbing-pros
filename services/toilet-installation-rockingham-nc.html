When Would You Require a New Toilet Installed?

Not all toilet issues will require a new toilet to be installed but there are circumstances where it would be a good option.

Replacing an Older or Broken Toilet

Older toilets can suffer from cracked porcelain, broken internal parts and continual issues with the toilet. Cracks can become visible on the toilet bowl or cistern which can cause issues with leaking and may not be able to be repaired.

If your toilet has broken significantly then it may be better to replace. A new toilet installation may be appropriate when the existing toilet is no longer suitable.

Constant Blockages & Flushing Issues

If you're suffering from constant blockages and flushing issues then there may be a problem. This can be caused by a blocked drain, poor flushing, scale build up or broken internal parts.

If you're experiencing continual issues and it's due to an old or broken toilet then it may be worth considering. Having your plumbing assessed can help to determine if it's an issue with your toilet or drainage.

Leaking Toilet

If there is water pooling at the bottom of your toilet, leaking from your toilet cistern or water appearing for no obvious reason then there could be an issue with a broken seal, loose fitting, broken toilet or other plumbing issues.

If your toilet is cracked or unable to form a good seal then it may need replacing. We will also assess the flooring to ensure your toilet will have a firm base to be installed on.

Bathroom Renovation

Another common reason for installing a toilet is if you're renovating your bathroom. This could be to change the style or look of your toilet.

If you're building from scratch then you'll need to ensure you have the correct drain, water supply and space for your toilet.

Types of Toilets

Choosing the correct toilet for your bathroom will depend on your bathroom layout, needs, design and plumbing.

Two Piece Toilets

These toilets have a separate tank and bowl. These are commonly found in homes and come in a range of styles, heights and flushing systems.

They can be easier to transport due to being separate but you'll need to assemble and seal the tank and bowl.

One Piece Toilets

These toilets have a single piece with both the tank and bowl. This can give a sleek look and less external connections.

This could be a good option if you're looking for a toilet that looks nice and is easy to clean. Ensure you have the right size and plumbing for your toilet.

Dual Flush & Water Efficient Toilets

Water efficient toilets use less water per flush. Dual flush toilets offer two flushing options.

Homeowners should consider how well the toilet works, if it will fit with your plumbing and what water efficiency standards it meets.

Comfort Height & Special Toilets

Comfort height toilets have a higher seat. If you prefer a higher seat then this could be a good option.

There are special Toilets Available such as elongated bowls, compact toilets etc. Ensure you have the right size toilet for your bathroom, accessibility and personal preference.

What Will Be Involved With Installing Your Toilet?

Depending on your current bathroom and toilet you'll need to carry out the following steps. If you're replacing your toilet then you'll need to:

Remove Existing Toilet

Turn off your water supply and empty your toilet tank and bowl. Remove the toilet.

You may find issues whilst removing your toilet such as broken flooring, broken seal and corroded fittings.

Inspection of Toilet Flange

The toilet flange connects your toilet to the drainage system and helps to secure your toilet to the floor. Toilet flange installation should account for the flange's position, condition and connection to the drain.

Before installing your toilet you should inspect the flange. If it's broken, loose, corroded or not in the correct position then you may need to repair or adjust it. A toilet flange installation diagram can help illustrate how the flange connects the toilet to the drain and floor.

Prepare Floor & Wax Ring

Ensure your toilet is being installed on a clean and firm surface. Place your toilet seal (usually wax or approved alternative) to help seal your toilet outlet and drain.

The correct seal will depend on your toilet design, flange height and installation.

Install New Toilet

Place your toilet over your drain and fittings. Ensure it sits evenly on your floor.

Tighten your fittings carefully following the manufacturers guidelines. If tightened too much it can cause damage to the porcelain.

Connect Water Supply

Connect your water supply to your toilet tank. Fill your tank and flush your toilet.

You should test your toilet. This will usually involve checking your water supply, toilet tank, flushing and if there is any leaking from the base. Any issues with your installation should be resolved before using your bathroom again.

Toilet Repair vs Toilet Replacement

Toilet repair and replacement are different. Depending on the state of your toilet you may need to repair or replace.

Toilet repair may be suitable if you have a small issue such as a worn flapper, faulty fill valve, loose handle or leaking water supply.

Toilet replacement may be better if:

  • The porcelain bowl or tank is significantly cracked.
  • There are continual issues with your toilet that can't be resolved.
  • You want to replace your old toilet.
  • Your toilet isn't suitable for how your household uses it.
  • You're renovating your bathroom.

Having a professional assess your toilet can help determine if there is an issue with your toilet, seal, flange or drain. If you replace your toilet without resolving the plumbing issue then you may still experience continual symptoms.

Common Toilet Installation Issues

Incorrect installation can cause issues with your toilet and may cause water damage.

Common issues include:

  • Incorrect Rough-In Measurements – Toilet doesn't fit with drain.
  • Poor Sealing – Damaged or incorrect seal can cause leaking.
  • Not Even – If your toilet rocks then this can put strain on your toilet and seal.
  • Over-Tightening – Too much force can cause cracking of the porcelain.
  • Leaky Water Supply – Incorrect connections can cause leaking.
  • Damaged Flange – A faulty flange can cause issues with your toilet.
  • Not Tested – Leaking or flushing issues can go unnoticed.

Following the toilet manufacturers guidelines and checking your plumbing can help prevent these issues.

FAQs About Toilet Installation in Rockingham, NC

How long does toilet installation take?

Toilet installation can take a few hours for a simple toilet replacement. If there are issues with your flange, flooring or plumbing then this can take longer.

Can I install a new toilet without changing my plumbing?

In many cases you can use your existing drain and water supply. However you should check your plumbing.

Should I replace my toilet flange?

Not necessarily. If your flange is in the correct position, firmly fitted and in good condition then you can reuse it. If it's damaged or not in the correct position then you may need to repair or replace it.

What is the difference between toilet installation and toilet replacement?

Toilet installation usually means fitting your toilet. This can include fitting in a new bathroom. Toilet replacement is removing your toilet and fitting a new one.

Can a new toilet help reduce water usage?

Water efficient toilets use less water per flush. How much water you save will depend on the toilet you choose, how much your household uses and how well your toilet is working.

Do I need a plumber to install my toilet?

Sometimes a knowledgeable homeowner can install a toilet. However if there are any issues with your connections, flange, leaking or unknown plumbing then it may be beneficial to have a professional plumber. Professional toilet installation may be helpful when the plumbing condition or installation requirements are uncertain.

What should I check after my toilet has been installed?

Ensure your toilet flushes properly, fills up, sits firmly and there's no leaking from the base, cistern or water supply.

Can I install my toilet in a different location?

In some cases yes. This can involve changing your drain, water supply, flooring and bathroom layout. This will depend on your property and plumbing codes.
