When Would I Require New Plumbing Piping?

There are a few different reasons why you may require new piping installed. Knowing why you need new piping can help you determine what piping system you need fitted with the correct materials.

New Builds

If you are having a new build then you will need to ensure your plumbing system can operate correctly with the layout of your building and fixtures. Water supply lines, drainage and vent lines will need to be planned.

Typically, new builds will require water lines to be installed to your bathrooms, kitchens, laundries and any other areas requiring plumbing. Drainage piping will also be required.

Damaged or Worn Piping

If your existing piping is causing frequent leaks, corrosion, cracking or other issues then you may require new piping installed. New piping can replace the damaged sections.

You may require a small section of piping replaced or larger sections of new piping.

Renovations

If you are renovating your bathrooms, kitchens or other areas of your property then you may require new piping installed. Moving fixtures may require new water supply lines, drainage lines and plumbing connections.

Your piping should be planned to fit the new layout.

New Fixtures or Appliances

New fixtures or appliances may require new water supply lines and drainage lines. Examples of this could be:

  • Kitchen and Bathroom Sinks
  • Toilets, Showers
  • Bathtubs
  • Washing Machines
  • Dishwashers
  • Water Heaters

Depending on your fixture/appliance and existing plumbing you will require different piping.

Types of Piping

There are a variety of different piping types required for different plumbing systems. Knowing what you require can help you determine what piping is needed for your project.

Water Supply Lines

Water supply lines are used to bring water from the main or approved supply to your plumbing fixtures/appliances. They will need to be the correct size for your water demand and pressure.

Water supply lines may include cold water lines, hot water distribution, branch lines and connections to plumbing appliances. It is important that they are connected correctly and with the correct piping materials to reduce the risk of leaks.

Drain Lines

Drain lines dispose of wastewater from sinks, toilets, showers, tubs etc. They will need to be the correct size, connected correctly and have the correct gradient.

Drain lines will need to be installed to fit with your fixtures and ensure the wastewater is flowing correctly. Incorrect planning can cause slow draining, frequent blockages or incorrect waste flow.

Sewer Lines

Sewer lines are used to dispose of wastewater from your property to the relevant sewer connection or approved wastewater system. This may involve sewer pipe installation, installing new sewer lines, connections or replacing sections.

Sewer lines will need to be carefully planned to ensure they are correctly aligned, sloped, connected and considering the site. The correct installation will depend on your property and existing sewer system.

Hot and Cold Water Lines

Hot and cold water lines are used to provide water to your fixtures. Hot water lines will be connected to your water heating system whilst cold water lines will provide water to your fixtures.

Depending on your installation you may require different piping materials, insulation, sizing and layout. Correct planning will ensure reliable water supply.

Whole House Repipe

A whole house repipe involves replacing a large section or all of the water supply lines in your property. This may be considered if you have a lot of wear and tear on your plumbing system, frequent leaks or old piping.

This may involve cutting into walls, ceilings, crawl spaces etc. The amount of work will depend on the layout of your building, where the piping is and the condition of your existing piping.

Piping Materials

Choosing the right piping for your plumbing is an important factor. There are a variety of different piping available with different connection methods.

PEX Piping

PEX (cross linked polyethylene) piping is commonly used for water supply. The flexible piping can be useful in certain areas of your building to reduce the amount of connections required.

PEX piping will require fittings and tools that are compatible. It should be used for the approved application, following manufacturers guidelines and plumbing requirements.

Copper Piping

Copper piping is commonly used for water supply. It is a stiff material so will require the correct cutting, connecting and support.

It may be suitable for certain hot and cold water applications. Consideration will need to be made for sizing, connecting, water and layout.

PVC & CPVC Piping

PVC piping is commonly used for drainage, waste and vent applications (where applicable). CPVC piping is used for certain hot and cold water supply applications.

They will require fittings that are compatible. The type of piping will depend on the application, temperature, pressure and local plumbing requirements.

Why Is Piping Installed Correctly?

Having your piping installed correctly can help your plumbing system to work correctly. Incorrect installation can cause leaks, reduced flow, drainage issues and damage to surrounding materials.

Caring For New Piping

Whilst new piping does require some care, regular maintenance can help you spot any potential issues.

Some helpful tips include:

  • Check any accessible piping and connections regularly.
  • Avoid putting too much pressure/weight on exposed piping.
  • If you notice anything unusual with your water pressure or drainage.
  • Avoid putting grease, incorrect materials or debris down your drains.
  • Follow any maintenance guidelines for your connected plumbing appliances.
  • If you are experiencing frequent leaks or unusual plumbing symptoms arrange for an assessment.

The correct maintenance will depend on your piping, system and property.

FAQs

How long will my piping take to install?

The time it takes to install piping will depend on the size, access and complexity. Short sections may be relatively quick to install whereas new builds or whole house repipes can take significantly longer.

What piping is best for my plumbing?

There isn't one piping that is best for all applications. PEX, copper, PVC and CPVC all have different uses. Consider your plumbing, approved applications and relevant regulations when choosing.

Do I need new piping for my renovation?

Not always but if you are moving your plumbing fixtures, adding new appliances or changing the layout of a room you may need new water supply or drainage connections. Assess your current plumbing to see what is required.

Can I have new piping fitted without cutting into my walls?

Some piping may be accessible via crawl spaces, utility areas or other areas. However if you need to install or replace hidden piping you may need to cut into your walls, floors or ceilings. This will depend on your building layout.

What is the difference between piping and repiping?

Piping can refer to adding new piping to a certain area or plumbing system. Whereas repiping usually refers to replacing your existing water supply piping in a larger section of your building, sometimes your whole property.

Do I need piping installed if I am adding a bathroom?

Typically yes as you will need connections for your water supply and wastewater drainage. This may involve installing new piping and connecting to your existing plumbing. This will depend on your property layout and relevant codes.

What affects the cost of plumbing piping in Rockingham NC?

The cost may depend on the material, size, length, access, labour, current plumbing and permits/inspections. An assessment will be required to determine how much is required for your project.

Should I have my new piping inspected?

This will depend on your installation and relevant local regulations. Plumbing systems should be tested using the correct methods. Any required permits/inspections should be carried out.
