When Would You Need A New Sink Installed?

You may need to get a new sink installed if you no longer have the right sink or you are doing a renovation. This will depend on your current sink, materials and if your plumbing can accommodate the new sink.

Damaged Or Worn Sink Basin

If the basin of your sink has cracks, chips, heavy staining, corrosion or damage, it can cause issues to keep it clean. If the damage is only minor then it may be possible to fix, however if it is causing issues with holding water or keeping the sink in place then it may need to be replaced.

You may want to get a new sink installed if:

  • There is a crack in the basin causing water to leak.
  • The sink is heavily damaged or worn.
  • The current sink cannot be fixed.
  • The mounting has been compromised.
  • It is difficult to find parts or compatible parts.

Consider the condition of your countertop, cabinet and plumbing.

Kitchens And Bathrooms

Kitchens and bathrooms often require changes to the sink, countertops, cabinets or overall layout. This means that you may want to replace your sink with something new to match the new look or improve functionality.

For example, if you are renovating your kitchen you may want to upgrade to a larger single bowl sink. If you are renovating your bathroom you may want to upgrade to a vessel sink.

Depending on the size or mounting of your sink, this can impact the cut out of your countertop, drainage, faucet and water connections.

Changes To Household Or Functionality

A sink you previously had may no longer be as convenient. Having a larger kitchen sink can help when washing large cookware. Utility sinks can give you a dedicated area for cleaning tools.

Consider how accessible it will be, how much space you have, how much storage you need and how easy it will be to clean. You should have a sink which will help with functionality and fit your available installation space.

Types Of Sinks Available

There are various types of Sinks Available to suit different areas and mounting.

Kitchen Sinks

There are a few common types of sinks:

  • Single bowl – One large basin for washing.
  • Double bowl – Two separate basins.
  • Drop in – Sits inside the cut out of your countertop with an overhang.
  • Undermount – Attached underneath the compatible countertop.
  • Farmhouse/apron front – Has an exposed front.
  • Workstation – May have extra features such as cutting boards, drying rack or built in tray.

Depending on your kitchen layout, cabinet size, countertop and how you want to use your sink will determine what is best for you. Kitchen sink installation and undermount sink installation will depend on the compatible countertop, cabinet and mounting requirements.

Bathroom Sinks

Bathroom sinks can be installed in a vanity, countertop or other area. Common types include:

  • Drop in – Sits inside the countertop cut out.
  • Undermount – Attached underneath the compatible countertop.
  • Vessel – sits above the countertop and may require a taller faucet.
  • Pedastal – Attached to a pedastal base.

Consider the depth of the basin, faucet, countertop space, storage and where the drain is located. Bathroom sink installation will need to account for these details.

Utility Sinks

Utility sinks are designed to be functional. They can be installed in your laundry room, garage, utility area or other area. They often have a deeper basin, durable material and different mounting.

Depending on what you want to use the sink for, the available space, cabinet/wall support and plumbing connections will dictate how it should be installed.

Choosing The Right Sink For Your Area

You should choose a sink which fits your installation area, works with your plumbing and provides good functionality for your property.

Size And Mounting

Measure up the area you have available for the sink. Consider the width, length and depth of the sink. How big the cut out of your countertop is and how much space you have underneath the basin.

Consider how it will be mounted. If you are having a drop in sink you may need a compatible cut out. Undermount sinks usually require a compatible countertop and secure mounting.

Consider the following:

  • How wide your cabinet is and how much space is available inside.
  • How big the cut out of your countertop is.
  • How deep the basin and sink is.
  • Where the existing drain is.
  • How many holes there are for the faucet.
  • How much space there is for the supply lines.

If you are having a larger or differently shaped sink, you may need to make changes to your countertop. This should be included in your installation.

Material, Durability And Style

Sinks are available in a variety of materials. Consider how easy they are to clean, how durable they are and what style you want.

Stainless steel is commonly used in kitchens and utility areas. Porcelain and ceramic are popular in bathrooms but can be used elsewhere. Composite and other materials are also available.

Consider:

  • How resistant to staining and scratching the material is.
  • How easy it is to clean.
  • If it matches your countertop.
  • How heavy it is and what support is required.
  • What the finish is like.
  • What you want to use it for.

The manufacturers specifcations can help you decide if it will work for your installation.

Faucet And Plumbing Connections

The new sink will need to work with your faucet. Some sinks have holes for the faucet, whilst others will require it to be mounted onto the countertop or wall. Kitchen sink faucet installation may be needed depending on the faucet and sink setup.

The drain hole will need to work with a compatible drain. The depth and shape of your sink can impact how the drain will line up and how much space there is for your P trap and connections. Kitchen sink drain installation will need to align with the sink drain and existing plumbing.

If your existing plumbing isn't compatible with your new sink, you may need to make changes. Ensuring it is compatible will help avoid any issues during installation.

What Is Included In A Professional Sink Installation?

This will depend on what type of sink you have, what you currently have and if you are making changes to your countertop or cabinet.

Measuring And Inspection

We will measure and inspect your area to ensure the new sink will fit. We may need to consider the cut out of your countertop, cabinet size, support, water connections, drain location and faucet.

Consider the condition of the surrounding materials. If there is any signs of damp damage, worn cabinetry or incorrect support then this will need to be addressed.

Remove Old Sink

If you are replacing your sink we will turn off the water supply and disconnect the faucet and plumbing. We will then remove the drain and mounting.

The old sink will then be lifted out of place. Depending on the type of sink this may involve unclipping, cutting through old seals or carefully lifting.

Be careful not to damage your countertop, cabinet or plumbing. Once it has been removed, we can inspect the installation area.

Mount New Sink

The new sink will be placed in the correct position, depending on the type of mounting. We will follow the manufacturers guidelines.

If it is a drop in sink we will place it into the compatible cut out and secure using the correct fittings and seals. Undermount sinks will need the correct support and will be attached to the countertop. Wall mounted and pedastal sinks will have their own support requirements.

Ensure it is correctly aligned so that it is secure and the faucet and drain holes are in the correct position.

Connections And Testing

Once the basin has been secured we can fit the faucet, drain and water connections. Under sink water filter installation may also require compatible connections if you are adding a filter.

We will then test the installation to ensure everything is working properly. We will check:

  • Hot and cold water is flowing.
  • The faucet is working.
  • No leaks from the water connections.
  • The drain is working.
  • Water is draining properly.
  • The P trap and accessible piping.
  • The sink is secure.

If there are any leaks or alignment issues, we will correct the connections or installation before using.

Bathroom sinks will need to be considered for the vanity/countertop, placement of the faucet, depth of the basin and how much storage there is. Vessel sinks may be higher and will require a compatible faucet.

Both should be considered for the existing water connections and drain. If you are changing the position of the sink you may need extra plumbing changes.

Sink Installation Vs Repair

Sink installation involves fitting a new sink into an area. This can be due to a renovation, new sink or replacement.

Sink repair involves fixing an issue with your current sink or connections. This can be due to leaks, damaged drain, loose mounting, issues with the faucet or drainage.

If you are looking for Sink Repair (Rockingham, NC) you may not need a new sink installed. If the basin is still structurally sound then it may just need repairing.

If the sink is heavily damaged, not suitable for the area or cannot be repaired practically then it may be better to have it replaced or installed. This will depend on the condition of the sink and connections.

A professional can help you decide what the best option is for your sink and property.

Common Issues With A Sink Installation

Issues can occur if the sink isn't installed properly. This can cause leaks, drainage issues, instability and can be difficult to use.

Common issues include:

  • Incorrect size for the countertop cut out.
  • Not enough support for the basin.
  • Incorrect drain alignment.
  • Incorrect seals.
  • Loose water connections.
  • Incorrect faucet.
  • Not considering cabinet space.
  • Not testing the installation.

Follow the manufacturers guidelines and requirements for the specific sink. If changes are needed to the countertop or cabinet ensure they are compatible and will provide the correct support.

FAQ'S

1. How do I choose the right sink for my kitchen or bathroom?

Consider the available space, cabinet size, countertop material, type of mounting, faucet and what you want to use it for. Ensure it works with your existing or planned plumbing.

2. Can I get a new sink installed without changing my countertop?

Often yes. If the new sink is the correct size and mounting it may fit into your current cut out. If you are changing the size or type of sink then changes may be required to the countertop.

3. Can I change the type of sink I have?

Sometimes yes. If you are changing from a drop in to an undermount or farmhouse sink changes may be required to the countertop, cabinet or support. This will depend on the installation area.

4. Does sink installation include faucet installation?

This will depend on what you require. Sink installation may include installation of the faucet and connections. We can confirm what is included.

5. How long does a sink installation take?

This will depend on what type of sink you have, how accessible it is, what you currently have for plumbing and if any changes are required to the countertop or cabinet. Generally a simple replacement won't take as long.

6. Should I replace my leaking sink?

Not necessarily. The leak may be coming from the faucet, drain, water connections or seals. Find out where the leak is coming from before deciding if it should be repaired or installed.

7. Can I install a new sink into my existing cabinet?

Yes if the cabinet is the correct size, has the correct support and space. If you are having a larger or deeper sink changes may be required to the cabinet.

8. What should I tell you when requesting a sink installation?

Tell us what type of sink you want, the model/size, where you want it installed, countertop material, faucet and if you need your existing sink removed. Including photos can help explain what you need.
