Signs that there may be a hidden leak

Water can leak from your plumbing but not cause an obvious puddle. It may leak from behind your walls, under your floorboards, in your ceilings or underground. Identifying early signs can help you decide to investigate further.

Unexpected increase in water usage

A sudden increase in water usage may mean that there is a leak. Small leaks can go on for a long time.

If your water meter is still running when no water is being used you may have a leak. This can give you a good indication. However, you should also consider irrigation, water appliances and other connections.

Dampness, Discolouration and Musty Smells

Water can travel through different materials and cause leaks from your pipework. This can cause:

  • Discolouration or water marks on your ceilings and walls
  • Damaged or damp floorboards, carpets or skirting
  • Peeling paint, bubbly plaster or damaged finishes
  • Musty smells in your rooms, cupboards or cabinets
  • Warped or squishy floorboards

However, this can also be caused by leaks from your roof, condensation, external water and issues with your appliances. A proper investigation can help determine whether it is caused by your plumbing.

Low water pressure or running water noises

If your supply pipework has an issue you may suffer from low water pressure. If the pressure changes suddenly or several of your fixtures have low water pressure you may need to investigate your plumbing.

You may also hear running water, hissing or dripping from your taps, toilets and appliances. Whilst this doesn't confirm there is a leak it can be a sign to look into further if there is unexpected noises.

Signs of leaks from your slab or underground

If you have a leak from your concrete slab or underground pipework it can be hard to identify. Potential signs include warm or damp areas on your floor, unexpected dampness near your foundations, repeated damp patches and high water usage.

Not all dampness on your floor will be caused by slab leaks. The source should be investigated using the correct leak detection methods.

Common reasons for hidden plumbing leaks

Leakages can occur in a variety of places. This can be caused by damaged pipework, broken connections, poor installation or damaged fixtures.

Old or corroded pipework

Over time pipework can become damaged. The material of the pipework, water conditions, installation and maintenance can cause the pipework to corrode. Eventually causing holes in the pipework.

Older plumbing systems can have a variety of pipework materials and previous repairs. This will help identify the section of your plumbing and whether it can be repaired or an overall plumbing investigation is required.

Loose connections or Damaged Fixtures

Not all leaks will be from your pipework. Water can leak from your supply connections, shut off valves, taps, toilets, water heater and appliances.

If there is a leak from a connection there may be dampness in your cupboard or around the fixture. This can be easily identified.

Slab leaks or damaged underground pipework

Water supply lines can run underneath your concrete slab or underground. If there is a leak from one of these pipes water can spread through the soil or materials.

This can be caused by damaged pipework, movement, broken joints or pressure. The cause and location must be identified to determine the best solution.

Temperature changes, pressure and worn pipework

Changes in temperature can affect your plumbing materials. High or fluctuating water pressures can cause stress to your pipework and connections. Over time this can contribute to leaks occurring.

An investigation will help identify where the issue is. However, it may require further investigation to determine why the leak has occurred.

Leak Detection Methods

The correct leak detection methods will depend on your plumbing system, suspected location, building materials and symptoms. A leak detection specialist may use one or more methods to help identify the issue.

Acoustic Leak Detection

Acoustic equipment can help identify the sound caused by water leaking from your pressurised pipework. A technician may listen to the sound of the water leaking from the pipework, fixtures or surface.

This can be useful if you have a water supply pipe that is hidden or underground. It depends on the leak, piping material, background noise and conditions.

Thermal Imaging

Thermal imaging equipment can identify temperature differences. If water is leaking it could cause a temperature difference in your wall, floor or ceiling. This can help identify an area to investigate further using a thermal camera.

Thermal imaging won't help you see through your walls or identify all leaks. There can also be other causes of temperature changes.

Moisture Detection & Pressure Testing

Using moisture detection can help identify if there is unusual moisture in your building materials. This can be used around walls, floors, ceilings etc.

Pressure testing can help identify if a section of your plumbing is maintaining pressure. If there is a loss in pressure this could indicate a leak or other issue. The testing method must be appropriate for your plumbing.

Electronic & Specialised Leak Detection Equipment

Sometimes specialised equipment will be required to identify leaks in your hidden or underground plumbing. Depending on your plumbing a technician may use electronic listening devices, tracking devices or other equipment to help identify the suspected leak.

There isn't one piece of equipment that will work for all properties. The access, pipework layout, material and type of water system will affect what equipment can be used to give useful information.

Leak Detection Process

Having a structured method can help avoid making assumptions. This can also help avoid cutting into your walls or floors unnecessarily.

Initial Investigation

Usually an investigation will start with a discussion about your symptoms. You may be asked when the issue started, if there has been a change in your water bill, which rooms are affected and if there has been any previous plumbing work.

The technician from a leak detection company will also look at any visible fixtures, supply connections, shut off valves and other plumbing. This will help identify where further testing can be carried out.

Testing & Identifying Leak

Once the likely area has been identified the technician will choose the correct leak detection methods. This can include acoustic equipment, moisture detection, thermal imaging or pressure testing.

This will help narrow down the suspected location. The level of accuracy will depend on the pipework layout, building materials and conditions.

Review & Repairs

Once the investigation has been completed the findings should be clearly explained. The technician will be able to explain where the leak is suspected to be, what evidence has been found and if they require any further access/testing.

This may include:

  • Fixing the leaking fixture or connection
  • Replacing the damaged section of supply pipework
  • Investigating or fixing the slab leak
  • Fixing the underground water line leak
  • Further testing

Leak detection will help identify where the leak is suspected to be. You should ensure what is included within the service. Further work may be required, including water leak repair services or pipe leak repair near me.

What to do if you suspect a water leak

Doing the following whilst arranging an investigation can help reduce further water damage.

  • Check fixtures. Check underneath your sink, around your toilet, water heater and appliance connections.
  • Check water meter. If possible, turn off any water using fixtures/appliances and check if your water meter is still running.
  • Know where your main shut off is. Know where your main water shut off valve is in case you have a large leak.
  • Move items. If it is safe to do so move items away from damp areas.
  • Note symptoms. Take note of when you started seeing dampness, what areas are affected and if there has been any changes to your water bill or pressure.
  • Tell the professional. Give them these details.

If there is water near electrical sockets, wiring or equipment avoid the area and seek the correct assistance. If there is a large leak and you can safely turn off the shut off it can help reduce damage.

Leak Detection vs Leak Repairs

Leak detection and repairs are related but different. Leak detection will help identify where the water is leaking from and provide information. Repairs will fix the damaged pipework, connections, fixtures or plumbing.

For example, acoustic testing can help identify where a hidden supply line leak is. However, you may still need to access the pipework and arrange water leak repair near me. If there is a slab leak there may be more decisions to make about how to repair it.

Ask if they offer both leak detection and repairs. Or if they will pass the findings onto another professional. Knowing what is included can help you plan what to do.

FAQs

How do I know if I have a hidden water leak?

Common signs include unexpected increase in water bill, damp areas, water discolouration, musty smells, low water pressure and running water noises. These can be caused by other issues so further investigation may be required.

Can I get leak detection without cutting into my walls or floor?

In many cases specialised equipment can help identify where the leak is suspected to be. However, some leaks will require some access or further investigation to confirm the exact source.

What is slab leak detection?

Slab leak detection is investigating potential leaks in your pipework that runs underneath your concrete foundation. This can be done using acoustic equipment, pressure testing and other suitable equipment to identify where the leak is suspected to be.

Can a water leak cause a high water bill?

Yes. If there is a leak from your supply line, fixture or underground pipework water will leak either constantly or occasionally. If there is an increase in your water usage without an obvious reason it can be a useful step to check for leaks.

How long will leak detection take?

This depends on where the leak is, how accessible it is, pipework layout and what leak detection methods are required. If the leak is easily accessible it may be quicker than a hidden slab or underground leak.

Will leak detection include plumbing repairs?

It depends on the service provider. Some may offer both leak detection and repairs. Others may just investigate. Ensure what is included within the service quoted.

Will thermal imaging identify all my plumbing leaks?

No. Thermal imaging can show temperature patterns that can help identify areas of concern. However it won't show all leaks. Other testing may be required.

Should I turn off my water?

If you can safely identify and turn off the correct shut off valve it can help reduce damage from an active supply line leak. If it is a small or unsure issue a professional can help decide if you need to isolate your water.
