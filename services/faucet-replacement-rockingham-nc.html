When Should I Replace My Faucet?

There are times when you will need to replace your faucet. Although sometimes just replacing a cartridge, washer, seal or other part inside can help, if the faucet is very worn you may want to replace it.

Non-stop Leak

If your faucet is regularly leaking then it may be due to worn parts inside, damaged valve seats or other parts that can be difficult to fix. This can lead to wasted water and be an annoyance.

You may want to consider replacing your faucet if;

  • It's still leaking after repairs.
  • Handles are hard to turn or aren't controlling the water correctly.
  • Damaged parts inside are difficult to get.
  • Leaking from multiple points.
  • It'll require a lot of repair work.

A professional can come out and help you understand if it's a simple fix by replacing parts or will require replacing the whole faucet.

Corrosion and Scaling

Faucets are regularly used meaning they are exposed to water, cleaning products and general use. They can become corroded, scale up, worn and damaged.

Sometimes scaling can be cleaned from around the aerator or water outlet, however if there is a lot of corrosion, cracked parts, damaged fittings or bad connections it may be best to replace.

If the faucet is leaking you will want to find out why. It could be due to the faucet itself, supply or pipework underneath. Without knowing why the faucet is leaking you may not solve the issue by replacing it.

Old Fixtures

Whilst your faucet may still be working it may not be the right style for your property anymore. If you're renovating your kitchen, upgrading your bathroom, making it more accessible or your requirements have changed you may want a new faucet.

An old faucet can give you the opportunity to have a new style handle, spout, finish and features. This will need to fit your sink, countertop and pipework.

Types of Faucets To Replace

Depending on where your faucet is and what piping you have you will need the correct replacement. Different faucets will have different fittings.

Kitchen Faucets

Kitchen faucets are used regularly for washing up, cooking, filling and cleaning your sink. There are a number of different types available;

  • Single handle – One handle to control the water flow and temperature.
  • Double handle – Separate hot and cold controls.
  • Pull down – Spray head that can be pulled down.
  • Pull out – Movable spray head.
  • Commercial – Often a taller, more flexible faucet.

When replacing your kitchen faucet, a kitchen faucet replacement will need to ensure it fits your sink. Consider how many holes you have, how much space you have on your countertop, how far the spout reaches and what connections you currently have. Larger or different faucets may not fit.

Bathroom Sink Faucets

Bathroom faucets can come in a few styles including centreset, widespread and single hole. This will depend on your sink or countertop and how many holes you have. Bathroom faucet replacement will need to take these measurements and fittings into account.

Ensure you have a faucet that will be easy to use, direct the water into the basin and give you enough space. The height and reach of the spout will be important for smaller or shallower sinks.

Bath & Shower Faucets

Bath and shower faucets can include a spout, control valve, handle and other parts. Bathtub faucet replacement and tub faucet replacement may involve these components. Sometimes you will not need to replace the visible part. Shower faucet replacement may also depend on the existing valve and fittings.

If the issue is with the internal valve, water temperature control or hidden pipework you may need extra work done. Check the current state of the faucet.

Touchless, Filtered, Wall Mounted & Other Specialty Faucets

Some property owners like to have touchless, filtered, wall mounted or other specialty faucets. These may require extra connections such as electricity or filters.

Ensure the faucet will fit where you want to install it.

Choosing The Correct Replacement

When choosing a new faucet you will want to ensure it will fit your current set up and connections.

Fitting

Firstly you will want to check how your current faucet is fitted. Your sink may have one, two, three or more holes. Some faucets will have a deck plate to cover the extra holes whilst others will need a specific fitting.

Important things to consider are;

  • Number of holes and how far apart.
  • Thickness of your sink/counter.
  • Space underneath.
  • Height of the faucet and how far the spout reaches.
  • Hot and cold water connections.
  • Current shut off valves and connections.

If you need a different fitting then you may need extra work on your sink/counter. Knowing what will fit can help avoid delays.

Material, Finish & Features

Faucets come in a range of finishes including chrome, brushed nickel, stainless steel and matte black. Choose the best option for your property, how easy they are to clean and what other faucets you have.

Consider how easy they are to use. Single handle faucets may be easier to use everyday. Double handles have separate hot and cold controls. Pull down sprays, touchless and other features can be useful depending on how you use your sink.

Ensure you have clear details on the product and that there are suitable faucet replacement parts. The manufacturers instructions can help you see if it will fit your current set up.

What Will Be Done During A Faucet Replacement?

Depending on what type of faucet you have, how worn it is and how easy the pipework is to reach you will have a variety of things done. Usually the following will be done;

Check Current Faucet

A professional will check your current faucet to find out why you need a replacement. They will look at the body, handles, fittings, connections, shut off valves and visible pipework underneath.

This will help them see if there are any issues such as corroded nuts, damaged shut off valves, old connections or signs of water damage inside the cabinet.

Remove Old Faucet

Shut off the water supply and drain any remaining water. Disconnect the connections.

Loosen the fittings and remove the old faucet. Older faucets can be harder to remove due to corrosion or scaling. Take care not to damage your sink, countertop or pipework.

Now you can check and clean the fittings.

Install New Faucet

Place the new faucet in line with the manufacturers instructions and secure using the correct fittings. Connect the connections to the hot and cold water. Add any seals or gaskets.

Turn the water back on and test the faucet. Ensure;

  • Hot and cold water is flowing.
  • Handles/controls are working.
  • Connections are not leaking.
  • Spout is working and any spray.
  • Water is going into the sink.
  • Check underneath for signs of damp.

If there is a leak you will need to check the connections or faucet.

Cost Of Replacing A Faucet

The cost of replacing your faucet can vary quite a lot. The cost of the new faucet is only part of the project.

Common reasons for this include;

  • Type Of Faucet – Simple bathroom faucets, pull down kitchen faucets and specialty faucets will all have different costs and installation.
  • Fitting – If your new faucet fits your current holes and connections it may not require as much extra work.
  • Difficulty Removing – Corroded fittings or an old faucet can make it harder to remove.
  • Connections – Damaged or incorrect connections may need replacing.
  • Shut Off Valve – Faulty shut off valve may need attention.
  • Extra Plumbing – Changing connections, fittings or hidden parts can affect the project.
  • Space – Not much space underneath can make it more complicated.

To get a useful estimate give us details on where your faucet is, what you have currently, what the issue is and if you have chosen a new one. The final project will depend on how it is installed.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. When should I replace my faucet?

If your faucet is heavily corroded, damaged, leaking constantly, parts are not available to replace or there are multiple faulty parts it may be more practical to replace. If it's a simple issue with a replaceable part inside then it may be enough to repair.

2. Can I replace my faucet without replacing my sink?

In many cases yes. If your faucet can fit with the fittings you will often be able to install a new one. Check the holes, thickness and space.

3. How long will it take?

Depending on the type of faucet, how easy it is to reach, how worn the fittings are and if there is any extra plumbing it can vary. Some can be quicker than others if there is a lot of corrosion or valve repairs.

4. Should I purchase a new faucet?

You can choose a new faucet but ensure it will fit your sink and pipework. If you aren't sure what model will fit ask for advice.

5. Can a leaking faucet damage my cabinet?

Yes. Water leaking from your faucet or connections can pool underneath your sink causing damage to your cabinet. Find out why it is leaking and sort it.

6. Are kitchen and bathroom faucets the same connections?

No. Different faucets can have different designs, fittings, connections and measurements. Check your current pipework and new faucet specifications.

7. Can I replace my shower faucet without opening up the wall?

Some shower trims and handles can be replaced without opening up the wall. However if there is a hidden valve or some plumbing issues you may need to open it up. This will depend on the design of your faucet.

8. What should I do if my shut off valve isn't working?

Don't force it as it can cause damage or a leak. Ask a professional to come out and check it. They will be able to tell you if it needs repairing or replacing.
