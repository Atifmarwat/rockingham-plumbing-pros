Types of Faucet

Depending on the area you are installing your faucet you may need a different type. Consider how you will use it, how you will mount it and whether your plumbing will allow for any connections.

Kitchen Faucets

There are several types of kitchen Faucets Available. You can have a standard spout, pull down, pull out and commercial style. Some have an extra spray function whilst others have a single handle.

Consider:

  • Height and reach of spout
  • Space underneath your cabinets and windows
  • Whether you would like a pull down or pull out sprayer
  • Number of mounting holes
  • Hot and cold connections

Even if the faucet looks good, if the spout isn't long enough to reach the basin or the handle doesn't move well it may not work well.

Bathroom Sink Faucets

Bathroom faucets are often installed on the sink or vanity countertop. Popular options include centreset, widespread, single hole and wall mounted.

Centreset faucets will usually have the spout and handles set together. Widespread faucets will have individual parts. Single hole faucets will usually be more compact.

The number of mounting holes in your sink is an important consideration. If you need a different setup for your new faucet extra work may be required.

Tub and Shower Faucets

These faucets will control the flow and temperature of your faucet. Depending on the faucet you may need to install a valve, trim kit, shower head, tub spout and other compatible items. Shower faucet installation will depend on the type of valve, trim kit and existing plumbing.

Some will have easy access to the plumbing however others will require work to be done inside the wall. If you have hidden valves you will need to pay particular attention to the compatibility, accessibility and wall.

Single Handle and Double Handle Faucets

Single handle faucets will have one handle to control the flow and temperature. Double handle faucets will have separate controls for the hot and cold.

Both can be suitable for kitchens and bathrooms. Often it comes down to a personal choice, whether it will fit your sink, space available and style.

Specialist and Touchless Faucets

Some faucets have added features such as motion sensors, touch, pull down sprayer, filtering and more. These may have extra installation requirements.

Some touchless faucets will require batteries or an electrical connection. If there is a filtering system then you may need an extra water connection and space underneath your sink. Always check the manufacturers requirements for installation.

Choosing the Right Faucet

By choosing the right faucet before installation you can avoid any compatibility issues and ensure your final result is functional.

Type of Faucet and Mounting

There are various types of Faucets Available. Deck mounted, sink mounted and wall mounted. Ensure that it will fit with your sink or countertop and there is enough space for the body, handles and connections.

If you are changing from 3 hole to single hole you should check that your new faucet has the correct deck plate or whether you will need to find an alternative for the spare holes.

Mounting Holes

The number of mounting holes will determine what type of faucet you can fit. Measure up your current setup and compare it to the manufacturers requirements. Also consider how thick your mounting surface is and how much space there is.

Material and Finish

Faucets come in a variety of materials and finishes. Chrome, stainless style, brushed and more. Consider what will go with your sink, cabinet handles and other fixtures.

Functionality is also important. In your kitchen you may want a pull down sprayer. In your bathroom you may want something compact.

Connections

Ensure your chosen faucet has the right connections, mounting and any accessories. Some faucets will have specific hose or connections.

If your faucet has a filtering system, soap dispenser or separate sprayer ensure your sink can accommodate it.

What Will Be Included in My Installation?

A typical installation will include preparation, removal, installation, connecting and testing. This will depend on the faucet and your existing plumbing. Professional faucet installation services may include these steps depending on the work required.

Check Existing Plumbing

The plumber will usually start by checking your sink or countertop. They will also check the mounting holes, shutoff valves and any accessible connections. This will help identify any compatibility issues.

They may find some corrosion, damaged shutoff valves, lack of access or worn connections.

Remove Existing Faucet

If you have an old faucet fitted the water will be turned off. The existing connections and mounting will be removed carefully to avoid damaging your sink or countertop.

You may need to clean out any old silicone, debris or dirt. The surface should be suitable for your new gasket.

Install and Connect New Faucet

Your new faucet will be placed in the correct position according to the manufacturers instructions. The mounting will be tightened and the correct hot and cold connections will be connected. This is an important part of kitchen sink faucet installation.

Some faucets will also require separate hoses for the sprayer, weights, electrical parts or filtering. These should be installed according to the product specification.

Test Water

Once everything has been connected the water will be turned on slowly. Test the hot and cold water, handle, sprayer and drainage.

Any accessible connections should be checked for leaks. Once the water is running you should check that there are no drips, strange movement or issues with the connections.

Kitchen and Bathroom Faucet Installation

Depending on the area you are installing your faucet you may have different functionality requirements. In your kitchen you may need a longer spout, more space for the sprayer and extra space underneath for the hoses. In your bathroom you may need something that fits a smaller sink and matches your vanity. You may also want to avoid splashing due to the incorrect spout position.

Ensure your shutoff valves and connections are in a good condition. If your existing valve doesn't shut off properly or your connections show signs of wear they may need attention.

If your property is being renovated it is important to ensure your faucet matches your countertop, sink and cabinets to avoid any issues with access.

Costs of Installing a Faucet

The kitchen faucet installation cost and overall faucet installation cost will depend on the type of faucet and how much work is required. A simple installation with existing compatible connections will be different to one requiring new connections or modifications to your sink/counter.

Common Issues with Faucet Installation

Incorrect installation can cause leaks, issues with functionality and damage to the surrounding area. To avoid this you should avoid:

  • Incorrect Faucet – Ensure the number of mounting holes and space match your chosen faucet.
  • Over tightening – Can cause damage to the fittings, threading or mounting.
  • Incorrect Connections – Ensure connections are connected to the correct hot and cold water.
  • Incorrect Sealing – Ensure your faucet is sealed using the correct gasket.
  • Worn Shutoff Valves – If your shutoff valves are worn this can cause issues.
  • Not Testing for Leaks – Once the connections have been made ensure they are not leaking.

By following the manufacturers instructions and checking your installation you can avoid any unnecessary issues.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long will my installation take?

This will depend on the type of faucet you are installing, how accessible it is and the condition of your existing plumbing. A simple installation may be quicker than one with old fittings, damaged valves or modifications.

Can I install a new faucet without replacing my sink?

In most cases you can. Just ensure that the sink has the correct number of mounting holes, thickness and space. Some modifications may be required depending on the type of faucet.

Can I install a single handle faucet where there was a double handle?

Sometimes. It will depend on how many mounting holes there are and how far apart they are. Some faucets may have a suitable deck plate to cover the spare holes. Always check the product specification.

Do I need to replace my connections?

Not always. If your connections are compatible and in a good condition they may be fine. However if they are damaged, corroded, leaking or incompatible then they may need replacing.

Can I install a touchless faucet in my existing kitchen?

Many touchless faucets are designed to fit existing sinks. However you will need to ensure it will fit with your mounting, space and electrical requirements. Some will have batteries and others may have different requirements.

Can I have my faucet installed during my bathroom renovation?

Yes. We can fit your Faucet During your bathroom renovation. It is important to ensure you know the specifications of your sink, vanity, countertop and faucet.

What should I do if my new faucet is leaking?

If it is safe to do so turn off the correct water supply. The leak may be coming from your connections, seals, hoses or internal parts. Identify where the leak is coming from.

Is Faucet Replacement different to installation?

Replacing your faucet will involve removing your existing one and fitting a new one. Installing can also mean fitting your faucet to a new location or part of a new sink or renovation.
