When to Consider Replacing your Toilet

There are a number of reasons that could indicate you should consider replacing your toilet.

Cracked Toilet Bowl or Tank

If your toilet is cracked it can affect the structural integrity of your toilet. Even if the crack is small water can start to leak through it. Cracks can get worse over time.

You should consider getting your toilet assessed as soon as possible. Depending on where the cracks are and how bad they are you may want to replace your toilet.

Toilet Clogs Frequently and Doesn't Flush Well

If your toilet is constantly clogging it can become frustrating. However, it doesn't always mean you need to replace your toilet.

This can be due to a blockage in your drain, build up inside your toilet, issues with your flushing mechanism or simply not working well for your household.

If your drain is clear and you are still having issues with your toilet you may want to consider replacing it. You should try to identify the underlying cause.

Leaking Around Toilet

Having water coming out of your toilet can be a sign that your toilet isn't sealing properly, it could be loose, damaged flange or other plumbing issues. It could also be due to the tank leaking either due to worn parts or a damaged tank.

If it is due to a seal that can be replaced you may just need it repairing. However, if it is due to a cracked toilet you may need it replacing. Depending on the cause, this may involve toilet wax ring replacement or toilet flange replacement.

You should also consider if the water has got onto your flooring. If you have a damaged or uneven floor installing a new toilet can cause further issues.

Old/Inefficient Toilet

Older toilets can use a lot more water per flush than modern water efficient ones. Property owners may want to replace their toilet to give their bathroom a modern look, improve function or choose a toilet with different features.

A new toilet can give you a modern flushing system, different bowl shape, improved sitting height and a toilet that fits your bathroom better.

Toilet Repair or Replacement?

Toilet repair may be suitable if:

  • Your toilet has worn flapper/fill valve.
  • Your toilet handle isn't working. This may require toilet handle replacement.
  • You need to work on your water supply.
  • Your toilet has a small issue. This may involve toilet flapper replacement or toilet fill valve replacement.
  • Your toilet hasn't cracked and is working well.

Toilet replacement may be more suitable if:

  • Your toilet has a large crack in the bowl/tank.
  • The toilet is having ongoing issues and has been repaired properly.
  • Your toilet is old and you want a new one.
  • The toilet isn't suitable for your household/property.
  • You are renovating your bathroom and want a different toilet.

Not all toilet issues will be solved by replacing your toilet. A blocked drain, damaged flange or water supply issues can still occur with your new toilet. It can be useful to know what the actual issue is to avoid extra work being done. Depending on the condition of the existing toilet, toilet valve replacement or toilet flush valve replacement may be considered before choosing a full replacement.

Choosing a Suitable Toilet

You will need to choose a suitable toilet for your bathroom and plumbing. There are a variety of different toilets to choose from depending on your property. If you are looking for a toilet replacement near me, it is important to consider the toilet's dimensions, features and compatibility with your existing plumbing.

One Piece or Two Piece

One Piece Toilets have a single tank and bowl. They tend to have a sleek design and fewer connections between the tank and bowl.

Two Piece Toilets have separate tanks and bowls. They are very common in residential bathrooms and come in a range of sizes and styles.

Depending on your bathroom style, size, ease of handling and toilet specifications you may wish to choose one or the other.

Water Efficient or Dual Flush

Water efficient toilets are designed to save water compared to many old toilets. Dual flush toilets give you different flushing options.

When choosing a water efficient toilet you should consider the flushing capabilities, water usage and plumbing. Choose a model that will work well in your bathroom.

Comfort Height or Elongated

Comfort height toilets give a higher sitting height. If you prefer to sit higher you may prefer these toilets.

Elongated toilets give a different sitting shape compared to round ones. They can be larger so make sure you measure up your bathroom to ensure it will fit and not cause any issues with doors, walls or other fixtures.

What is Involved in Toilet Replacement?

Depending on your toilet model and current setup there will be a variety of tasks involved. Generally you will:

Remove Old Toilet

Turn off the water supply and empty the tank and bowl. Undo the water supply and fittings.

Carefully remove your toilet to minimise any damage to your bathroom floor, drain connection or surrounding areas. You may need to dispose of your old toilet appropriately.

Check Flange and Drain

The flange connects your toilet to your drainage.

After removing your old toilet check that your flange is undamaged, not rusty, loose or incorrectly placed. Check the drain for any obvious blockages or issues. A toilet flange replacement may be required if the existing flange is damaged or cannot provide a secure connection. A toilet flange installation diagram can also help explain the flange's position in relation to the toilet and drain.

If your flange is damaged it may need fixing or replacing. A new toilet cannot guarantee to fix a faulty drain connection.

Prepare Area

Ensure the area underneath your toilet is clean. Your floor should be sturdy.

If your old toilet was leaking there could be water damage to your flooring. Any necessary repairs should be done.

Choose the correct seal ring or approved seal. Toilet wax ring replacement may be needed when the existing seal is damaged or unsuitable.

Install New Toilet

Place your toilet over the drain connection and bolt. Ensure it is evenly sat on the floor.

Fix the fittings to tighten the toilet. Be careful not to over tighten as this can damage the porcelain.

Ensure your toilet is level and stable.

Connect Water Supply

Connect your water supply and fill the tank. Flush your toilet to ensure it is flushing well.

Check your toilet for any obvious leaks around the tank, water supply and base of the toilet. If there are any issues with your toilet leaking, rocking or flushing you should fix them before using your toilet regularly.

Common Issues with Toilet Replacement

Issues can occur if your toilet isn't suitable for replacement.

Common issues include:

  • Damaged Flange – Your toilet may not fit securely or seal properly.
  • Incorrect Size – Toilet may not fit the rough-in or bathroom.
  • Uneven Floor – Toilet may rock.
  • Sealing – Water may leak around the base.
  • Over Tightening – Can cause toilet to crack.
  • Rusty Shutoff – Water supply may need fixing.
  • Damaged Floor – Previous leaking may have caused damage to the installation.

It is important to address any issues you discover when removing your toilet as your new toilet may have the same issues.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long will toilet replacement take?

Toilet replacement can take a few hours for a simple toilet. If there are any extra issues with the flange, flooring or plumbing it may take longer.

Should I replace my toilet if it is constantly clogging?

Not necessarily. It can be due to a blockage in your drain or other plumbing issues. An assessment can help identify if it is due to your toilet or drain.

Can I replace my toilet without changing the drain?

In many cases you can replace your toilet using the existing drain. Ensure it is the correct rough-in size and compatible with your plumbing.

Do I need to replace the flange?

No. If your toilet flange is secure, correctly positioned and in good condition you can reuse it. If it is damaged or incorrectly positioned you may need it fixed.

Can I choose a different style of toilet?

Yes. As long as the toilet fits your bathroom and plumbing. Ensure it is the correct measurements and compatible.

Is removal included?

Generally toilet removal will be included. However, it can vary. Ask if removal and disposal is included in your estimate.

Will my new toilet use less water?

A water efficient toilet can use less water per flush than an older toilet. It will depend on what toilet you choose and how often you use it.

Can I replace my toilet if my bathroom floor is damaged?

You should assess your flooring and carry out any necessary repairs. It is important that your toilet is installed on a stable surface.
