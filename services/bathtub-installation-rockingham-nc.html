When Should I Install a New Bathtub?

A bathtub can be installed as part of a larger bathroom renovation or to improve your bathroom. The reason for the installation will impact which type of bathtub you will want and how much preparation will be required. Whether you are planning a replacement or new bathtub installation, it is important to consider the space, plumbing and preparation involved.

New Bathroom

A new bathroom gives you more flexibility over the location of your bathtub. You will need to consider the water supply, drainage and space on the floor.

Before choosing your bathtub think about how it will fit with the toilet, basin, shower and door. Think about where the plumbing will be going so your chosen bathtub doesn't cause any surprises.

Old or Damaged Bathtub

Your bathtub may be showing signs of age with cracks appearing, the finish becoming worn, staining becoming an issue and being hard to keep clean. Sometimes they can also cause issues with leaks or structural concerns.

This can give you the opportunity to change the size, material and style of your bathtub. Before installation think about any moisture damage, worn floor and plumbing issues.

Renovating Your Bath

Sometimes you will want to change the style of your bathtub. This could include changing a shower/bathtub combination as part of a bathtub shower installation, moving the bathtub or choosing a freestanding bathtub.

You may need to adjust the drainage, water supply, floor and finishes on the walls. Ensure the layout you want is feasible before purchasing any materials.

Types of Bathtubs

There are several types of bathtubs available. They will all have different space and installation requirements and will require different maintenance.

Alcove Bathtub

An alcove bathtub is installed within a 3 sided space. They are commonly used in bathrooms with a shower and bathtub combo.

Alcove bathtubs come in various lengths, widths and depths. Choose a size that will fit the space you have available and ensure your walls have the right finish and protection.

If you want to shower in your bathtub think about where you will put your shower head, what finish you want on your walls, how you want to arrange your shower curtain/door.

Freestanding

A freestanding bathtub will be freestanding and can be a feature in your bathroom. They come in various shapes and sizes.

This style will require you to have enough space around the bathtub. You may also need to consider how you will get the plumbing connected.

Ensure your floor can hold the weight of your bathtub when full.

Drop-in and Undermount Bathtubs

A drop-in bathtub is designed to fit into an existing platform or decking. The rim sits on the surrounding surface. The platform can be finished with matching materials.

An undermount bathtub sits below the surrounding decking. The bathtub will need to be specifically designed for this. The structure and waterproofing will need to be carefully planned.

They will need to be accurately measured.

Soaking and Access Bathtubs

A soaking bathtub will give you extra depth when you are bathing. Ensure the overall size will fit, the capacity of water and the height to enter the bathtub. Also ensure the floor can support the weight.

Access bathtubs may have lower entry points, grab rails, seating or other features to make it easier to use. Choose the correct setup based on your needs and the space you have. Walk in bathtub installation may also require additional consideration of the available space, access and product specifications.

Some specialist styles may need extra plumbing or electrical. Ensure you know what is required by looking at the product specifications.

Choosing the Right Bathtub

Ensure your bathtub will fit in with your bathroom and users. By comparing sizes, materials and plumbing requirements before ordering you can avoid any issues.

Measuring Up

Carefully measure up the area where you want to install your bathtub. Ensure you know the length, width and height available. Also think about where other fixtures, doors and storage are.

Think about how you will get the bathtub into your home. If you have a large bathtub it may not fit through a small doorway, hallway or staircase. This can affect which models will be practical to install especially for freestanding and one piece bathtubs.

Ensure there is enough space to reach your taps, clean around and any shower enclosure or curtain.

Materials

Common materials for bathtubs include:

  • Acrylic – Usually lightweight with lots of shapes and finishes available. Ensure you clean according to the manufacturers instructions to avoid scratching the surface.
  • Fibreglass – Often used for budget bathtubs. The finish may need some care.
  • Porcelain Enamel Steel – Has a smooth finish over the steel. If it gets chipped you will see the steel underneath.
  • Cast Iron – Heavy and durable with an enamelled finish. Ensure the floor can support the weight.
  • Solid Surface – Available in certain modern styles. The weight, care and installation will vary.

Consider the durability, weight, looks, ease of cleaning and product specification.

Plumbing, Drainage and Taps

Ensure your bathtub will work with the plumbing in your bathroom. Ensure you know where the drain is, overflow, taps, water supply and any access panels. Bathtub drain installation must also be considered to ensure the drain and overflow are compatible with your chosen bathtub.

Some bathtubs have left or right hand drainage. If you get the wrong one it can cause issues when installing.

Depending on the bathtub you may need to have different taps. Wall, deck or floor mounted fillers may be required.

Installation Process

Depending on the style of bathtub, existing bathroom and whether you need any plumbing work, the installation process will vary. The installation of bathtub fixtures will involve several steps. A typical installation will include:

1. Measuring Up

The installer will look at the layout of your bathroom and ensure the size of the bathtub will fit. They may look at the floor, existing plumbing, drainage, walls and access.

Look at the installation instructions for your chosen model to ensure you know how to support, space and connect.

2. Remove Existing Bathtub

If you are replacing your bathtub you may need to disconnect and remove the existing bathtub. Depending on the installation of the bathtub you may need to open up the surrounding walls.

Once removed you can look at any issues with the area. This can include moisture damage, damaged framing, damaged floor and plumbing issues. Make sure any issues are addressed before placing your new bathtub.

3. Plumbing and Drainage

Ensure the drain and overflow will work with your new bathtub. Ensure the water supply and taps can connect.

If the new bathtub has a different drainage location or taps you may need to alter the plumbing. This will depend on the existing layout and how easy the connections are to reach.

4. Position and Support

Place your bathtub according to the manufacturers installation instructions. Depending on the product you may need to have a support bed, framing and level.

Ensure your bathtub is held securely in place. If not supported correctly it can put strain on your bathtub, drainage and surrounding finishes.

5. Connect and Test

Once your bathtub is in place connect the drain, overflow, taps and water supply. Ensure there are no leaks and your bathtub drains correctly.

Check the surrounding areas. If your bathtub is touching the walls or decking ensure it has the correct finish and seal.

Replacing Your Bathtub During a Bathroom Renovation

Replacing your bathtub can be an important part of renovating your bathroom. Some people will want to keep the same layout and choose a similar sized bathtub. Others will use it as an opportunity to change the overall style.

If you are replacing your bathtub with a similar size you may not need to make many changes to the layout. However if you are switching from an alcove to a freestanding bathtub you may need to make some changes to move the connections and prepare the floor.

Think about:

  • Whether you can remove your existing bathtub without causing too much damage to the walls and floor.
  • Whether the new bathtub will fit in with the space.
  • Whether the drainage and overflow will work.
  • Whether the floor needs repairing or extra support.
  • Whether the surrounding wall finish or shower will be changed.
  • Whether the plumbing connections will be accessible.

By fully assessing your project you can decide whether it will be a simple replacement or part of a larger renovation.

Cost of Installing a Bathtub in Rockingham, NC

The bathtub installation Rockingham NC cost will depend on what you choose and how much work is needed to prepare your bathroom. The overall bathtub installation cost can vary depending on the project. A basic replacement may be very different to installing a freestanding bathtub, moving plumbing and renovating.

Common costs include:

  • New finishes – New tiling, wall paneling, bathtub decking or shower.

  • Taps, drainage, overflow and other features – These will impact the overall project.

Ask for an estimate based on your bathroom and chosen bathtub. Ask what materials and labour are included. Will they remove your existing bathtub? How will they deal with any unexpected repairs?

Getting Your Bathroom Ready

By getting your bathroom ready you can make the installation run more smoothly. Talk to your service provider about how you will get access, materials delivered, turn off the water and what to expect.

Useful things to do:

  • Remove all toiletries, towels, storage and personal items.
  • Ensure there is a clear path to where the bathtub will go.
  • Ensure the bathtub will fit with the layout and drainage direction.
  • Talk about whether they will remove your existing bathtub and construction materials.
  • Ensure surrounding rooms and flooring are protected.
  • Ensure there will be limited access to your bathroom.
  • If you share your bathroom with multiple people in your household arrange an alternative bathing option.

Common Issues

As there are many parts to installing your bathtub you need to be careful to avoid any issues. By planning carefully you can avoid any unnecessary issues.

Common issues include:

  • Incorrect measurements – The bathtub may not fit in the space. This can cause you to change products or do more renovating.
  • Drainage issues – The drainage may not fit with your existing plumbing.
  • Not enough support – Ensure your bathtub is supported according to the manufacturers instructions. This is especially important if it is full of water.
  • Not sealed – If the edges and connections to the walls aren't sealed properly water can get to the surrounding materials.
  • No access – If the bathtub won't fit through the access it can cause issues when delivering and installing.
  • No leak test – Ensure the drainage, overflow and supply don't leak before closing off the surrounding access.

If you need any plumbing or structural changes ensure you know what the local requirements are. Ensure you follow the product instructions and any building and plumbing regulations.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long will it take to install my bathtub?

This will depend on the type of bathtub, access and how much preparation is required. If you are replacing with a compatible product it may be quicker than if you are doing any demolition, plumbing changes, floor repairs or wall finishes. We can talk through how long your project will take once we have assessed your bathroom.

Can I replace my bathtub without changing the plumbing?

Sometimes. If your new bathtub will fit with the size, drainage, overflow and taps then you may be able to use your existing plumbing. If you are changing the style or location you may need to make some changes.

What is the easiest bathtub to install?

This will depend on your bathroom and product. A standard alcove bathtub may be easier to install as it will fit with the existing space and plumbing. Larger freestanding bathtubs, bespoke platforms and specialist bathtubs may require more preparation.

Can I install a bathtub in a small bathroom?

Yes, as long as the bathtub will fit in the space and you have good access to the surrounding fixtures. Small alcove bathtubs and some smaller styles may be suitable. Ensure you measure up and check the door will fit.

Do I need a new drain for my new bathtub?

Not necessarily. If the existing drain will fit with the new bathtub it can be used. Depending on the product the drain may need replacing or adjusting.

Can I install a freestanding bathtub where my existing alcove bathtub is?

Possibly. You will need to consider the floor, drainage, water supply, taps and available space. As the style is different you may need to change the surrounding finishes.

What should I do if I find water damage after removing my existing bathtub?

Investigate where the issue is coming from. If the floor, framing or wall materials are damaged they may need repairing. If there is a plumbing leak this should be fixed. Covering up the damaged materials with a new fixture will not solve the issue.

Should I choose my bathtub before starting my renovation?

It is usually helpful to choose your bathtub to ensure it will fit with your bathroom. This means you can plan the layout and plumbing based on the actual product. Ensure you know the size, drainage direction, tap requirements and installation instructions.
