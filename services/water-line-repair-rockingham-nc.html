Common Signs of Issues

Sometimes issues with your water line may not be obvious. However knowing the signs can help your property owner investigate.

Low Water Pressure

If you are noticing a drop in water pressure throughout your property it may be an issue with your water supply line. This could be caused by a leak, blockage, faulty valve or issues with your incoming water.

If you are noticing issues with just one fixture then it may be an issue with that one fixture and its branch piping. If several fixtures are having issues then you may want to have a general plumbing assessment.

Puddles/Wet Areas

If you are noticing unusual wet areas in your garden, around your building or at your plumbing access points then it could be a leaking water line. Leaks in underground piping can cause water to pool above the ground or soak into the surrounding soil.

However it could also be caused by irrigation, drainage issues or other issues. You should consider where the wet areas are and what could be causing them.

Discolouration

Brown, yellow or cloudy water can be caused by disturbed sediment, corrosion, plumbing issues or changes to your water supply. This does not necessarily mean that your water line is damaged.

If you are regularly getting unusual water appearance/quality then don't assume it is just an issue with one pipe. You may need to have an assessment to see where the issue is coming from in your plumbing or with your water supplier.

High Water Bill

If you are suddenly getting higher charges for your water usage then it could be caused by a leak. If there is constantly water leaking from your water line then you may be losing water.

Compare your recent usage to previous bills and check for any visible signs of leakage.

Noisy Water

If you are hearing water running when no fixtures or appliances are in use then it could be a sign. This could be caused by a leak, running fixture or plumbing issues.

Considering where the noise is coming from and when it is happening can help identify the issue. However you may want to have it professionally tested.

Common Causes of Damage

There can be several reasons why issues are occurring with your water supply piping.

Corrosion

Some piping can suffer from corrosion over time. This can cause the pipe, joints and connections to degrade.

Depending on the condition of the material and level of damage it may be better to repair a small section.

Ground Shift

Changes to the ground can put pressure on your underground piping. This can cause cracking, misalignment and other issues.

The repair may involve identifying the section that is damaged and considering the surrounding conditions.

Tree Roots

Tree roots can grow towards damp areas underground. If they reach vulnerable joints or damaged piping then they can cause the piping to shift or degrade.

Damage caused by tree roots may require digging to access the piping.

Freezing

If the temperature is low enough then the water in your piping can freeze. This can cause cracking and faulty connections. Water lines can also be damaged by impact or construction.

Consider what has caused the damage so you can choose the right repair.

Old Piping

Old piping can suffer from worn connections, weak materials and unreliable components. Faulty or worn joints can cause leaking.

An assessment can help identify if it is just an issue with one joint.

Types of Repairs

There are various types of water line repairs that can be carried out. It is important to identify what line is causing the issue and where it is located.

Main Water Line

This will supply water into your property from the relevant connection. Damage to your main water line can affect several fixtures and may require main water line repair.

Repairs may include identifying where the leak is, accessing the damaged piping and fixing the section. Who is responsible for this may depend on how your property is set up and your local utility regulations.

Underground Water Line

Often underground piping can be hidden under soil, gardens, driveways etc. This can make it difficult to identify where the leak is without the correct testing. Underground water line repair may require digging to access the damaged section.

You may need to dig to access the section. How much digging and reparation is required will depend on how deep the piping is and what is around it.

Water Supply Pipe Leak

A leak can occur in your water supply pipe. This may be an easily accessible internal piping or hidden section. Repairs may include replacing the damaged section, fixing the connection or faulty fitting.

The correct repair will depend on the type of piping, pressure and level of damage.

Connections/Fittings

Connections, joints and fittings are used to connect different sections of your water supply. If the connection is loose, damaged or degraded then it can cause leaking.

The connections should be repaired using compatible components and methods. If the piping around the connection is also damaged then simply replacing the fitting may not be the right solution.

How Issues are Diagnosed

Having an accurate diagnosis can help prevent unnecessary repairs. Depending on the issue you may want to have a plumbing assessment completed. This can include:

  • Reviewing the symptoms reported.
  • Checking accessible piping, valves and connections.
  • Identify if it is just one fixture or several areas.
  • Look for any visible dampness, wet soil or signs of leakage.
  • Use the correct leak detection methods if the piping is hidden/underground.
  • Identify where the damage is likely to be and how much.

Depending on the type of piping, access and symptoms the diagnosis may vary.

Types of Repairs

The type of repair should depend on the condition and location of the piping. A solution that would work for an accessible internal pipe may not be the right solution for a buried main line.

Spot Repairs

Spot repairs can be used to repair a specific section of damaged piping. This may be suitable if there is just a small leak and the rest of the piping is in good condition.

The damaged section/connection can be accessed and repaired using compatible components.

Replace Section

If there is cracking, severe corrosion or the piping is no longer suitable then it may be suitable to replace a section.

This can help with spot damage without needing to replace the whole water line. However you should still consider the condition of the rest of the piping.

Trenchless Repairs

Depending on the type of piping, damage, site and technology some underground water line projects can be completed using trenchless methods. This can reduce the need for constant digging.

However not all water line issues will be suitable for trenchless repairs. A professional assessment will be needed to identify if it is technically possible.

When to Replace

If there is a lot of degradation, multiple recurring leaks or extensive damage then it may not be the most practical solution to repair. You may want to consider replacing some or all of the piping.

Consider the condition of the piping, how it will be used in the future, access and how big the project will be.

Urgent Issues

A large leak in your water line can cause a lot of water loss and damage. If you suspect you have a leak that is flooding your property then you should take reasonable steps to minimise the impact and get the correct assistance. This may require emergency water line repair depending on the severity of the leak.

  • Turn off the main water valve (if it is safe and you know where it is).
  • Avoid anyone going near any areas where water could come into contact with electricals.
  • Don't go into any flooded areas with electrical risks.
  • Protect your items from water if possible.
  • Note where the suspected leak is and what symptoms you are experiencing.
  • Contact the correct plumbing professional or water utility depending on where the issue is and who owns the line.
  • Don't dig up any underground water lines unless you know where other underground utilities are.

Prevention

Whilst you may not be able to prevent all piping failures you can try to keep an eye on your plumbing to identify any potential issues.

  • Check for any visible leaks or corrosion in accessible piping/connections.
  • Be aware of any sudden pressure changes.
  • Monitor your water usage for any unexpected increases.
  • Fix any recurring plumbing leaks.
  • Protect vulnerable piping from freezing.
  • Don't put heavy weights or dig near underground piping without checking where the utilities are.
  • Follow any maintenance advice for plumbing equipment connected to your water.

If your property has old piping or has had recurring water line issues then having a general plumbing assessment can help identify if further repairs or replacement should be considered.

FAQs

What are the signs of a broken water line?

Signs can include low water pressure, unexpected wet areas, high water bill, discolouration or noises when no fixtures are in use. As these can be caused by other issues you should have the line checked.

How do I know if my main water line is leaking?

If there is a leak in your main water line it can cause low water pressure throughout your property or unexpected wet areas near your underground connection. However this could also be caused by other plumbing or supply issues. Have an inspection and use the correct leak detection methods.

Can I repair my water line without digging?

Depending on the type of piping, location and damage some water line issues can be repaired with minimal digging. However some will require digging to access the area. Not all projects will be suitable for trenchless repairs.

Should I repair or replace my damaged water line?

If there is minimal damage and the rest of the piping is in good condition then it may be suitable to repair a small section. If there is a lot of degradation, recurring issues or extensive damage then it may be suitable to replace. This will depend on the condition of the piping and project.

How long will it take to repair my water line?

This will depend on where the issue is and how bad the damage is. If it is an accessible pipe then it may be a simpler repair than an underground one that requires digging, difficult access or site repairs. Consider how big the project will be.

What causes underground water line leaks?

Possible causes can include corrosion, ageing materials, ground movement, tree roots, freezing, damage and degraded connections. This will depend on the piping and surrounding conditions.

Is a water line leak an emergency?

If there is a large leak causing flooding, affecting your water supply or causing a risk to your property then you may want to get it attended to quickly. If there is water near any electrical risks then you should prioritise your safety and don't go into the area.

What affects the price of water line repairs in Rockingham NC?

The price will depend on where the leak is, type of piping, how much damage there is, how accessible it is, if digging is required and if it needs repairing or replacing. An assessment will need to be carried out to identify how much work will be required on your property.
