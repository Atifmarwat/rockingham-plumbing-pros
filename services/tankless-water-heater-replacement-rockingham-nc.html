When Should I Replace My Tankless Water Heater?

Tankless water heaters heat your water on demand. They can provide you with long lasting services, however after some time you can expect components inside your water heater to start to break down. You may need to replace your tankless water heater if you are experiencing regular issues.

Regular Issues

If your tankless water heater is regularly suffering from issues like ignition failure, error codes, fluctuating temperatures and breaking down of components then you may need to look into replacing your tankless water heater. Whilst one issue won’t necessarily mean you need to replace your whole system, regularly having issues could mean that it’s more practical to replace.

You may want to consider replacing your system if;

  • Multiple components have broken over time.
  • Cost of repairs are getting expensive.
  • It keeps cutting out.
  • Parts are hard to get for an older model.
  • Your system isn’t providing reliable services despite previous repairs.

Getting a professional to come and check your system will help determine if it’s a simple component that can be replaced.

Low Performance

If your tankless water heater is struggling to provide you with consistent hot water, it could be due to low capacity. Other reasons could be limescale build up, heat exchanger issues, incorrect settings and other mechanical issues.

If you are experiencing inconsistent hot water whilst showering, doing laundry or washing up then you should find out why. Having a correctly sized system will help with the capacity issues. However if your current system can be repaired then you may not need to replace it.

Age

Tankless water heaters tend to last longer than traditional water heaters, however how long they last will depend on the quality of the water, maintenance, usage, installation and quality of the equipment.

If your system is old and components are starting to degrade you may need to replace it. You may benefit from replacing your system with a new model offering improved controls, efficient running and better features for your property.

Common Signs

Spotting the signs early can help you plan ahead.

Common signs include;

  • Regular error codes – Could indicate ignition, sensor, heat exchanger or other issues.
  • System is regularly cutting out whilst running.
  • Fluctuating temperatures.
  • Low hot water output.
  • Strange noises.
  • Visible leaks.
  • Corrosion – Can affect safe and reliable running.
  • Regular repair issues.

Not all of these signs will mean you need to replace your water heater. Some could be due to maintenance, low flow, electrical issues or other repairable issues. Getting a professional to check your system will help.

If you have a gas water heater and you can smell gas then you should vacate the area and contact the correct emergency gas service. Do not turn any switches on/off and do not attempt to repair.

Flow Rate & Temperature Rise

Tankless water heaters are often referred to by their flow rate and temperature rise.

Flow rate refers to how much water can be heated at once, this is often measured in gallons per minute (GPM). Temperature rise refers to how much the temperature rises from the incoming water.

If you have a large temperature rise required your flow rate may be lower. The maximum flow rate advertised may not be achievable based on your properties requirements.

Consider the temperature rise and flow of your fixtures.

Should I Replace Or Repair My Tankless Water Heater?

Deciding to replace your tankless water heater can be a big decision. In some cases it may be cheaper to repair your system to get it working again. However in other cases it may be more practical to replace.

Consider replacing if;

  • Your water heater is fairly new.
  • Issue can be fixed by replacing a component.
  • Heat exchanger and main components are in good condition.
  • It still meets your properties hot water requirements.
  • Expected to fix the issue and provide reliable services.

Issues such as faulty sensor, ignition component or maintenance related issues may be repairable.

Consider replacing if;

  • It keeps breaking down.
  • Main components have suffered a lot of damage.
  • Doesn’t have enough capacity.
  • Older system that has required multiple expensive repairs.

Consider how old the equipment is, what repairs are needed, how it will function in the future and how much you are investing in replacing it.

Maintaining Your New System

Regularly maintaining your tankless water heater will help keep it functioning well.

How much maintenance you need will depend on your model, water quality and usage.

Limescale Build Up

Over time minerals can build up in your tankless water heater. This can cause issues with the heating and reduce flow.

Some systems will need to be flushed or descaled on a regular basis. How often will depend on the water and manufacturers instructions.

Checking Components

Regular checks can help spot any leaks, degraded connections or other issues. Gas models may need checking for combustion components and venting whereas electric will need checking for electrical components.

Any technical or safety related maintenance should be done by a qualified professional.

Cleaning Filters

Some tankless water heaters have filters on the inlet. These can collect debris, so cleaning or servicing them according to the manufacturers instructions can help keep the flow correct.

Monitor Performance

Keep an eye out for regular error codes, strange noises, fluctuating temperatures and unexpected cut outs.

By addressing any developing issues early it can reduce the risk of further issues with your equipment. Keep your installation and maintenance documents to hand so future professionals can see what model it is and what it requires.
