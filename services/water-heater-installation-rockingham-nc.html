Choosing the Right Water Heater

The first consideration for installing a new water heater is choosing the type of system that best suits your home. This depends on the storage capacity, energy source, installation and how much hot water will be needed. A system which would work for one house may not provide enough hot water for another.

Traditional Storage Tank Water Heaters

Traditional storage tank water heaters heat and store hot water in an insulated tank. When hot water is needed, the water heater will use the hot water which has been stored.

Tank water heaters come in various sizes and can be either gas or electric. Many people will be familiar with tank water heaters. If you know how much hot water is needed for your household, it may be the right choice.

Consider how many people are using the hot water in your home, how often people shower, and if there are any appliances such as dishwashers and washing machines which could be using hot water. Choosing the right sized water heater will help avoid running out of hot water when needed.

Tankless Water Heaters

Tankless water heaters heat the water as it flows through the unit. This can provide hot water on demand depending on the heating capacity and the temperature of the water coming into the unit. Tankless water heater installation will depend on the capacity and requirements of the selected unit.

Tankless water heaters can be a good option if you want a different system or don’t have much space for a large tank. However the unit will need to be the right size for how many fixtures are likely to be in use.

However it may not always be the best option for your property. Consider if there is the correct fuel source, electrical connection, water flow, ventilation and if it will be an expensive install. Also consider how much maintenance will be needed.

Gas or Electric Water Heaters

Gas and electric water heaters both have different installation requirements. Gas will need the correct gas connection, correct ventilation and the right amount of combustion air. Electric will need the correct circuitry. Gas water heater installation and electric water heater installation each require the correct utilities and connections.

The best option will depend on the utilities you have, the infrastructure of your home, the specifications of the equipment and your preferences. If you would like to switch between fuels, it may require extra work. Consider all the installation requirements.

Choosing the Right Size Water Heater

Choosing the right size is important to ensure you have a constant supply of hot water. If it is too small it may struggle to provide enough hot water when needed. If it is too large it can increase the cost of the equipment and running.

Household Size

The amount of people in your home will be a starting point. It is important to consider how people use hot water in your home. For example, if multiple people shower in the morning.

Consider:

  • How many people regularly use hot water.
  • How many showers/baths could be used in a short period of time.
  • Are there multiple bathrooms in use?
  • How often is the dishwasher and washing machine being used?
  • Are there large baths or high demand fixtures?

This will help decide whether a traditional tank or tankless system is the best option.

First Hour Rating & Flow Rate

For traditional storage water heaters, the first hour rating will give you an idea of how much hot water the unit can provide during high demand periods. This takes into account the amount of hot water being stored and how quickly it can be heated.

For tankless systems, the flow rate and temperature rise are important. The unit must be able to heat enough water to provide for the amount of fixtures in use. If the unit cannot provide enough hot water for the overall demand, it could cause inconsistent temperatures.

Compare the specifications of the equipment to what you need.

When to Install a New Water Heater

You may want to install a new water heater if your current system is no longer reliable or your hot water demands have changed.

Replacing an Old/Faulty Unit

Over time a water heater may become less reliable due to the parts inside wearing down. If you are having regular issues, seeing signs of rust, leaking from the tank or not enough hot water even with the settings set correctly, it may be time to think about replacing it.

If the tank is leaking, this can show that the inside of the tank is wearing down. It may be more practical to replace the unit.

Consider the condition of your current unit. The type of fuel, size, dimensions, connections and ventilation can all affect what new units will be suitable.

Increasing Capacity/Improved Efficiency

If you have added more bathrooms, different numbers of people or increased your hot water demand, you may need a different system. Upgrading your system can mean you can choose a unit which is more suitable for your current demand.

Many people will compare the energy efficiency ratings and running requirements of new units. Consider the efficiency of the unit when choosing the new unit. This should be considered along with the price, installation, expected usage and maintenance.

Planning for a Remodel/New Property

If you are adding a bathroom, remodelling your kitchen or building a New Property, you may need a different hot water system. Planning ahead can help ensure the connections, electrical/gas, ventilation, drainage and space is correct.

If you are remodelling it is important to check if the current system can cope with the additional fixtures. Choose a new unit based on what is needed for your property.

What is Involved with Professional Installation?

A well planned installation will ensure the unit is suitable for your property. It will be installed according to the manufacturers guidelines.

Review Current System

Generally the installation will start by reviewing your current water heater and surrounding area. This should include considering the space, fuel/electrical connection, water connections, drainage, ventilation and ease of maintenance.

The size and condition of your current system can help decide what new unit to choose. If you want to upgrade to a tankless system, you may need to check additional infrastructure.

Prepare the Installation Space

Before fitting the new unit, the area may need preparing. Consider the size of the unit, servicing space, shut off controls and the manufacturers guidelines.

Depending on the system, you may need to adjust the water lines, gas lines, electrical connections, ventilation and drainage.

Connect the Unit

The new water heater should be connected to the correct utilities and plumbing. This may include connecting the hot and cold water, shut off valves, temperature and pressure relief, drainage and fuel/electrical connections.

Depending on the unit, the installation can vary. Gas and electric units will have different requirements. Tankless units may require additional modifications.

Final Checks

Once installed, check the system according to the manufacturers guidelines. This can include checking for any leaks, ensuring it is working correctly, temperature controls and correct safety measures.

Gas units should have correct ventilation and combustion requirements. Electric units should have correct electrical connections. The homeowner should also be given basic information on how to run and maintain the unit.

Gas & Electric Requirements

The utilities currently in your property can heavily impact what type of water heater you can install.

Gas

A gas water heater will require the correct gas connection and approved ventilation. The installation should follow the manufacturers guidelines, combustion air requirements and safety requirements.

The new unit may not fit with the same ventilation and connections as the old unit. Ensure it is suitable.

Electrical

An electric water heater will require the correct electrical connection. This should match the voltage, amps and wiring requirements. A larger or different unit may not be suitable for the current circuit.

Ensure the electrical requirements are considered. Incorrect wiring can cause safety risks and issues with the running of the heater.

Water Connections & Safety

The water heater should be connected with the correct water connections. Depending on the unit and requirements, this may include a temperature and pressure relief valve, discharge pipe, drainage and expansion control.

This will depend on your property, system and local regulations.

Tankless Requirements

Installing a tankless unit may require some extra planning. The unit will need enough fuel or electricity and must provide the right amount of hot water flow. Tankless hot water heater installation will also depend on the selected model and its installation requirements.

Gas & Electricity

A gas tankless unit may require a gas line with enough gas to meet the demand as stated by the appliance. An electric tankless unit may require a high electricity requirement and suitable circuits. Electric tankless water heater installation may therefore require checking whether the existing electrical supply and circuits are suitable.

Check the current utilities match the requirements of the chosen model. If upgrades are needed they should be included.

Ventilation & Water Flow

Tankless units will have specific ventilation and positioning requirements. The unit should be installed to allow it to run according to the manufacturers guidelines. It should also be easily accessible for servicing.

The unit should be able to provide the correct temperature rise for the expected water flow in your home. Hard water or mineral build up may affect maintenance requirements so this should be considered.

Tank to Tankless

Changing from a tank to tankless may require some changes to the plumbing, gas/electricity, ventilation and mounting. The connections from the previous tank may not be suitable.

Before choosing to change consider how much work will be involved in the installation and what benefits you will get from it. A professional can help determine if it is suitable for your property and what changes will be needed.

Cost of Water Heater Installation

The cost of installing a water heater in Rockingham will depend on what unit you choose and what work will be carried out. The water heater installation cost and tankless water heater installation cost will depend on the equipment and installation requirements. Key factors can include:

  • Type – Traditional tank, tankless, gas and electric all have different requirements.
  • Size – Larger units or units with higher outputs can have different costs.
  • Connections – If the connections are suitable they may be easier to use.
  • Fuel – Changing between gas and electric may require additional electrical, gas or ventilation work.
  • Position – If the unit is difficult to reach, has unusual dimensions or needs moving can affect labour.
  • Regulations & Safety – May require updates or changes.
  • Removal of Old Unit – May include disconnecting the old unit.

Ask for a clear breakdown of what unit will be used, what work will be carried out and if there will be any additional changes. Comparing the full installation can be more useful than comparing the unit prices. If you are researching home depot water heater installation cost, compare the full installation requirements and included work rather than looking only at the unit price.

Getting Your Home Ready

A little preparation can make the installation easier. Before the installation, you can:

  • Clear any items stored and obstructions around the existing unit.
  • Ensure the installation area is accessible.
  • Note any known leaks or issues with the current system.
  • If you have chosen a model, have the information ready.
  • Discuss any changes to bathrooms, appliances and hot water demand.
  • Ask how the old unit will be disconnected.

Do not try to disconnect any gas lines, electrical wiring or pressurised plumbing. This should be done by the correct service professional.

Maintaining the New Water Heater

Maintenance can help ensure your new system is running in a suitable condition. The type of maintenance and how often will depend on the model, water quality and manufacturers guidelines.

For traditional tank units, you may need to check the temperature and pressure relief valve, connections and anode rod (if applicable). Sediment management may also be recommended.

For tankless units, you may need to descale or flush periodically. This depends on how much mineral build up there is. Filters, vents and other parts should be checked according to the manufacturers guidelines.

Keep your owners manual and installation information to hand. If you notice any unusual noises, leaks, inconsistent temperatures or error messages, arrange an assessment.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long will it take to install my water heater?

It will depend on the unit and current system. If you are just replacing the unit it may take less time than if you are moving it, changing fuel, adding new ventilation or upgrading electrical. A more accurate estimate can be given by the installer once they have viewed the property.

What size do I need?

The size will depend on how many people are in your household, how many fixtures are being used and peak times. For traditional tank units we look at the size and first hour rating. For tankless units we look at the flow rate and temperature rise.

Should I choose tank or tankless?

A tank unit will store hot water for your household. A tankless unit will heat the water as it flows. Consider your hot water demand, available utilities, installation requirements, space and budget.

Can I change from gas to electric?

It may be possible however it can require upgrading your electrical. Check the electrical capacity of your property and unit specifications.

Will I need a permit for a new water heater?

This will depend on the project and local regulations. Ask the installer or local building department what permits and inspections are required.

What should I check?

Check for any leaks, connections, running and safety. Gas units should have correct ventilation. Electric units should have correct connections.

How long will my new water heater last?

This will depend on the type of unit, quality of installation, water quality, usage and maintenance. Follow the manufacturers guidelines for caring for your unit. If you start to see signs of deterioration, arrange an assessment.
