Shower Types

You'll need to choose the right shower system for your bathroom. Consider how big your bathroom is, budget, how much maintenance you want, and how you want to use your shower. Different systems will need different preparation.

Preformed Shower Units

Preformed shower units are manufactured to fit together. They can come as one unit or separate base and wall. They can be made from a variety of materials depending on the product. Acrylic, Fibreglass, and other manufactured materials.

These can be a great option if you want a simple design. As they can have less grouting than a traditional tiled shower. They come in a variety of sizes, shapes, colours, and designs depending on the manufacturer.

Before purchasing a preformed shower unit you'll want to measure up your bathroom. Consider how big your doorway is, where your drainage is, and what the installation instructions say. If you're installing a one piece shower it may be difficult to get into your bathroom. However, if you're installing a multi-piece shower this can give more flexibility.

Custom Tiled Showers

Custom tiled showers give more flexibility with the size, layout, patterns, and finishes. You can design your shower to fit an unusual bathroom shape or to have certain features such as niches, seating, and decorative tiles. Shower tile installation will need to be planned around the chosen design.

Tiled showers will need to be prepared carefully. Ensure that your subfloor, waterproofing system, shower base, and drainage are compatible with your installation. Don't assume that your tiles and grouting will be your waterproofing.

As there are a few elements involved with custom showers you'll want to plan your design and prepare.

Walk In/Accessible Showers

A walk in shower can make it easier to get into your shower. Depending on your bathroom and your needs you can have a low threshold entrance, extra grab bar reinforcement, seating, and extra space. Walk in shower installation and walk-in shower installation will need to account for the available space and accessibility features.

A shower without a threshold may require some changes to your floor and drainage. When designing your shower you'll want to ensure that you've planned in any accessibility features, including shower grab bar installation where required.

Shower Components

Shower Pan/Base

Shower pan/base collects the water and drains it away. Shower pan installation needs to account for the shower base, support, and drainage.

Tiles/Panels

Tiles/panels give your shower its finished look and protect your prepared shower.

Shower Door

Helps reduce water splashing and separates your shower from the rest of the bathroom. Shower door installation and glass shower door installation need to suit the shower enclosure and available space. Shower glass installation also needs to be compatible with the chosen design.

Valve & Trim

Controls the temperature and flow.

Shower Head & Fittings

Provides the desired shower pattern.

All of these will need to be compatible. Consider the measurements, drainage, wall alignment, and manufacturers instructions.

Choosing the Right Shower for Your Bathroom

Not all showers that look great in the showrooms will be the best option for your bathroom. Consider how you'll use your bathroom and what is required.

Bathroom Size

Measure up your bathroom to see how big your shower will be. Consider how big your toilet, vanities, doors, and other fittings are. Ensure your shower fits without causing unnecessary restrictions.

Consider how the materials will fit into your bathroom. If you have narrow hallways, stairs, and doorways this can affect how big your preformed shower units are.

Materials

Depending on the material you choose you'll need to consider how easy it is to clean and maintain. Preformed showers made from acrylic or fibreglass will not require grouting like tiled showers. Tiled showers give more design flexibility but you'll need to ensure that your grouting is cared for.

Choose finishes that will look good, be durable, easy to clean, and compatible with the rest of your bathroom. Follow the manufacturers instructions.

Water Supply

Before choosing your final shower design you'll need to consider your existing plumbing. Depending on your water supply connections, valve, drainage, pipework condition, and water pressure this can affect your shower.

If you're having a new shower head or fitting installed it may not solve the pressure issue. If you have a low or inconsistent water flow this could be due to your water supply, valve, or pipework causing issues.

What Is Involved With a Professional Shower Installation?

Depending on whether you're having a new bathroom, direct replacement, or full upgrade you'll have different work involved. Professional shower installation services typically include:

1. Survey & Measurement

The installer will survey your bathroom. They will measure your shower area and find out what shower you want. They may also look at your water supply, drainage, flooring, walls, and plumbing.

This helps identify any potential issues.

2. Demolition

Depending on whether you're replacing your shower you may need to remove your existing shower. The installer will be able to look at the area and see if there are any issues with the materials, dampness, damaged framing, or plumbing.

Any repairs will need to be done to the surrounding area before your new shower is put in place.

3. Water & Drainage

Ensure your water supply and drainage will work with your chosen shower. You may need to adjust your pipework, reposition or replace your shower valve, adapt your drainage, or fix other issues. Shower drain installation may also be required as part of the drainage work.

Install your connections following any requirements and manufacturers instructions. Ensure your drainage works with your shower base and flooring.

4. Shower Base & Wall Prep

Ensure your shower base is well supported and will direct water to your drainage. If you're having a tiled shower you'll need to ensure your shower has the correct waterproofing system, transitions, and slope.

Install your shower panels/tiles following your chosen design. Pay particular attention to the corners, plumbing, niches, and shower base/wall connection.

5. Fittings, Enclosure & Checks

Once your surfaces are ready you can fit your shower head, valve trim, handles, doors, and other fittings. Shower door installation cost will depend on the selected door and fitting requirements. Ensure your shower is working well with the correct water flow, drainage, and no leaks.

Check your shower has everything secured, sealed, and any adjustments needed from your product instructions.

Shower Repairs

Not all shower issues will require a full replacement. If your shower head isn't working, handle is worn, connections are leaking, or the valve isn't working this may be able to be repaired.

Shower Repairs in Rockingham, NC can be done. If your shower is having an issue with just one component this may be a good option. However, if there is a lot of damage, constant issues, or it doesn't fit the layout of your bathroom then it may be better to replace your shower.

If there is water damage behind your shower, damaged shower base, or issues with the surrounding area you may need more work done. An inspection can help you decide whether it would be best to repair, partially upgrade, or fully replace your shower.

Factors That Affect the Cost of a Shower Installation in Rockingham, NC

The cost of your shower will depend on what shower you're having installed and how much preparation is needed. A simple replacement may cost less than a custom tiled shower or changing the layout. Shower installation cost and shower installation Rockingham NC cost will depend on the scope of work and materials required.

Common factors that can affect the cost are:

  • Type & Size of Shower – Preformed showers, custom tiled showers, and large showers will have different material and labour costs. Walk in shower installation cost will also depend on the size and preparation required.
  • Piping – Moving your drainage, changing your water supply connections, or moving your valve can add to the cost.
  • Demolition – Existing materials may need to be removed.
  • Water Damage & Repairs – There may be hidden damage once the shower is removed.
  • Waterproofing & Wall Prep – Depending on your shower you'll need the correct subfloor and waterproofing.
  • Doors & Fittings – Glass shower doors, upgraded valve, shower head, and fittings.
  • Access – If there isn't much access to your bathroom this can affect how long the installation takes.

Ask for a quote for your project. This will help you understand what work is being done, what materials are being used, and any conditions that may affect the project. Don't compare quotes based on the cost of the shower unit.

Preparing Your Bathroom

Having a little preparation can make the installation easier. Talk to the professional about when the work will be done, how you'll have access, materials being delivered, and protecting surrounding areas.

  • Remove any personal items, toiletries, and bathroom fittings.
  • Ensure there is a clear path from the entrance.
  • Know where your main water shut-off is.
  • Know which fittings/bathroom areas won't be available.
  • Talk to them about what they'll do with your old shower and any materials being removed.
  • Ensure you've checked the size of your shower components and fittings.
  • If it is a shared bathroom in your home plan an alternative layout.

Why Is It Important To Have a Good Installation & Waterproofing?

A shower is constantly exposed to water so it's important that your shower base, walls, drainage, and connections work well together. If your joints aren't prepared well, the materials aren't right, or the drainage isn't correct then you may have damp getting into areas that shouldn't be.

Waterproofing is especially important if you have a tiled shower. Ensure you have the correct waterproofing system and follow the instructions to ensure your corners, joins, plumbing, and shower base work well with your shower.

A good installation will also consider ventilation. Use your bathroom extractor fan if you have one, follow the instructions, and fix any damaged seals or signs of dampness.

FAQs

How Long Will My Shower Take To Install?

This will depend on what shower you're having and how much preparation is needed. A simple shower replacement may take less time than a custom tiled shower. If there are changes to your piping, repairs, or changing the layout it may take longer. Ask for an estimated time frame.

Can I Replace My Bath With a Walk In Shower?

In many bathrooms you can replace your bath with a walk in shower. It will depend on the size of your bathroom, piping, drainage, flooring, and what design you want. A professional can help you decide what changes may be needed.

Are Preformed Showers Better Than Tiled Showers?

It will depend on your bathroom. Preformed showers can give you a simpler surface with less grouting. Tiled showers give more flexibility with the size and design. It will depend on what design you want, how much maintenance you want, installation requirements, and your budget.

Will I Need To Change My Piping?

Not necessarily. If you're having a shower with a compatible layout and the existing piping is in a good condition you may be able to use some of the connections. If you're moving the shower, changing the valve, drainage, or fixing existing piping issues you may need to make changes.

Can I Get A Shower Installed In A Small Bathroom?

Yes! There are some compact showers available. It will depend on the size available, how big your doorway is, where your fittings are, and what access you need. It's important to measure your space.

There Is Water Damage Around My Shower

Don't cover the area until you know what the issue is. It could be that your fittings, piping, drainage, shower base, or walls are causing the water to leak. Assess the cause and how bad the damage is.

Should I Choose My Shower Fittings?

It's a good idea to choose your shower, valve, trim, shower head, and shower enclosure early. Some products will have specific compatibility and rough-in requirements. This will help ensure that you don't order the wrong components.

Should I Repair My Shower Or Have A New One?

This will depend on how bad your shower is. If it's just an issue with one of your fittings/valve it may be something that can be repaired. If there is a lot of damage, constant damp issues, or it doesn't fit your layout it may be worth replacing. An assessment can help you decide what options you have.
